# Fields rectify, shapes don't: Tesla-valve graphene verdict

**Bottom line.** A Tesla-valve-shaped two-terminal graphene channel cannot rectify by geometry alone in the ballistic regime, and nothing in the published record suggests it will beat a well-designed funnel or Z-diode once both are modelled under the same physics. For any elastic two-terminal scatterer, unitarity of the scattering matrix forces T_SD(E) = T_DS(E) at every energy, so the linear-response I–V is odd in V regardless of shape; the only rectification available is the even-order term G₂V², and Christen–Büttiker showed that a gauge-invariant G₂ exists only through the bias-induced redistribution of the electrostatic potential *inside* the device ([Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)). Every solid-state Tesla valve that has actually rectified—graphene electrons at 20–100 K with diodicity ≈1.05 ([Geurs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04862)), a GaAs 2DEG above a ≈300 µA threshold ([Sarypov et al. 2026, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16443)), graphite phonons at 45 K ([Huang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11525177))—did so in the *hydrodynamic* regime and showed none in the ballistic or diffusive limits, so the Tesla geometry has no demonstrated ballistic mechanism beyond what any asymmetric shape offers, while its loops add series resistance in both directions. The best-documented two-terminal benchmarks are the Nguyen et al. hBN-encapsulated devices (γ = 1.58–1.66 at 60–80 µA, full dimensions ±5 nm, λ ≈ 0.7 µm) and the Truccolo/Lemme 2026 CVD device (fully dimensioned, near-ohmic null case); the record 1.97 asymmetry of the 23–26 nm-neck KAUST device is abstract-only and sits in the few-mode quantum regime (λ_F ≈ 35 nm at 10¹² cm⁻²), where classical trajectories are invalid at the neck. The practical consequence for the proposed simulator is structural: a field-free event-driven ray tracer is provably unable to produce any I–V asymmetry and is useful only as a geometry/contact/detailed-balance validation tool; the minimum rectification-capable model needs an in-device potential (uniform field at least, Poisson preferably) plus a momentum-relaxation model, and the analytic straight-line free flight is partly lost once the field is on. The defensible programme is therefore a general geometric-diode optimizer in which Tesla-inspired shapes are one parametric family, built in narrow stages with numerical acceptance gates, plus a separate decision on whether to pivot to electron hydrodynamics (20–100 K in hBN-encapsulated graphene), the one regime where the Tesla geometry has a real mechanism and where finite-element Navier–Stokes expertise applies directly.

## Reciprocity forbids shape-only rectification; only the bias-induced potential can break it

The single equation that settles the ballistic question is the two-terminal Landauer–Büttiker current, I = (g e/h) ∫dE T(E;{V}) [f_S(E) − f_D(E)], with g = 4 for graphene. Christen and Büttiker write the general multi-terminal form with A_αβ = Tr[1_α δ_αβ − s†_αβ s_αβ] and note that the sum rules Σ_α A_αβ = Σ_β A_αβ = 0 "follow from the unitarity of the scattering matrix" ([Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)). For two terminals those two sum rules give A_SS + A_DS = 0 and A_SS + A_SD = 0, hence **A_SD = A_DS, i.e. T_SD(E) = T_DS(E) at every energy**. This uses flux conservation only, not time-reversal symmetry, so it holds in a magnetic field, for any shape, for specular or disordered elastic walls, and for any number of modes; Song states the identical identity for arbitrary B ([Song 1999](https://www.nano.physik.uni-muenchen.de/kotthaus/_assets/pdf/1999/99-09_Song_PRB.pdf)), and Löfgren et al. summarize the practical content: "G12(B) = G21(B). The reciprocity theorem breaks down in the non-linear response regime" ([Löfgren et al. 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0309106)). Pictures of electrons being "funnelled" one way and "bounced back" the other cannot change this: the classical limit of unitarity is Liouville's theorem and detailed balance, so at equal chemical potentials the phase-space flux S→D equals D→S mode by mode.

The Knudsen-flow analogue is the same theorem in gas-kinetic language, and it is worth stating because the user's premise leans on it. For free-molecular flow through any channel, the NASA analysis of converging/diverging tubes derives the forward/reverse transmission relation from equilibrium alone: under steady state "the arrival rates ... anywhere within the enclosure, must all be equal" and "there must be no net flow through the tube, or slot", which yields a relation that "does not depend on the shape of the opening, or passageway" ([Richley & Reynolds, NASA TN D-2330](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19640011357/downloads/19640011357.pdf)). In the textbook form this is A₁W₁₂ = A₂W₂₁: a Tesla channel with specular or cosine-law walls has equal molecular conductance both ways at equal pressure. Ratchet-shaped Knudsen channels pump only when a temperature difference is imposed; with ΔT = 0 the second law forbids a net flow, otherwise "it would be possible to extract work simply by cooling a single heat bath" ([Shahabi et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5269730/)). Diffuse (Lambertian) walls do not help either—the cosine law is precisely the detailed-balance-preserving boundary, and it emerges even from pure specular reflection in chaotic geometries ([Celestini & Mortessagne 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.1117)). So the Knudsen regime (Kn ≫ 1) and the fluid Tesla valve (Re ≳ 200, Kn ≪ 1) are analogies for two mutually exclusive transport regimes, and only the latter rectifies; in the fluid case diodicity is ≈1 up to Re ≈ 100 and turns on only with inertial/turbulent flow ([Nguyen, Abouezzi & Ristroph 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8128925)).

What is not forbidden is the second-order term. Expanding I = G₁V + G₂V² + …, Christen–Büttiker show that for two terminals gauge invariance leaves a single rectification coefficient G₁₁₁, given by G_αβγ = (e³/h)∫dE(−∂_E f)∫d³x [δA_αβ/eδU(x)](2u_γ(x) − δ_βγ), where the characteristic potentials u_γ(x) = ∂U(x)/∂V_γ solve a Poisson equation driven by the injectivity (partial local density of states) of each contact. For a quantum point contact with two screening regions the result is G₁₁₁ = (e²/h)R(∂_{U₁}T − ∂_{U₂}T) in the screened limit and G₁₁₁ ∝ (D₂ − D₁)∂_E T in the unscreened limit, "proportional to the asymmetry of the density of states"; it "vanishes for a symmetric barrier" ([Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)). Two consequences matter for a modeller. First, geometric asymmetry of hard walls enters G₂ only through *where injected charge piles up* (D₁ ≠ D₂, e.g. at a neck) multiplied by the sensitivity of T to the local potential or to energy; it never acts alone. Second, if one takes the equilibrium T(E) and integrates over a bias window with a symmetric split, I(V) = (2e/h)∫T(E)[f(E − µ − eV/2) − f(E − µ + eV/2)]dE is exactly odd in V; any even term obtained by an asymmetric split (all of eV on one lead, or a linear drop imposed by hand) is an artefact of a non-gauge-invariant assumption about where the potential falls. Sánchez and Büttiker put it directly: "a self-consistent description of charge redistribution is crucial" ([Sánchez & Büttiker 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0404387)). This is exactly the criticism Büttiker's school levelled at Fleischmann–Geisel's mode-counting explanation of the Song rectifier, and at Dragoman's taper model, which assumes V_j = jeV/(N+1) across the taper and obtains rectification entirely from that assumed profile ([Dragoman & Dragoman 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.3760)).

### Reconciling the "geometry-only" asymmetry of Truccolo 2024 with reciprocity

The research notes contain an apparent conflict that must be resolved before any simulator design, because it decides what the simulator has to contain. The methods analysis argues that a non-interacting hard-wall billiard with no in-device field and no momentum relaxation cannot rectify, while Truccolo et al. report that their "geometry-only" specular, ballistic model yields **A ≈ 1.05–1.15 at low bias**, rising toward a ceiling W_d/W_n at very large bias ([Truccolo et al. 2024](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)). Both statements are correct, and the reconciliation is in Truccolo's kinematics: the "geometry-only" case is *not* field-free. Their free flight integrates dk/dt = −qE/ħ with ds/dt = v_F k/|k| under a **uniform in-plane field E_x** (a particular, non-self-consistent choice of the bias-induced potential), so trajectories curve between wall collisions and the funnel transmits the field-aligned distribution differently for the two field signs. The paper states the field-free limit explicitly: at E_x = 0 "a symmetrical transport condition is expected, resulting in T_DS = T_SD = W_n/W_C" = 0.1, and the symmetric control device gives A = 1 at all biases ([Truccolo et al. 2024](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)). This is the Christen–Büttiker mechanism with the characteristic potential chosen by hand as u(x) = x/L. When the Udine group replaced the uniform field with a self-consistent 3D Poisson solution (graphene sheet, 100 nm oxide, back gate), "the asymmetry coefficient has almost halved", and for a shallow funnel angle α_d = 7.6° A dropped slightly below 1 because electrostatic attraction toward the neck tips exceeded the geometric enhancement; their conclusion is that uniform-field analysis "tends to overestimate the rectifying capabilities" and electrostatics "can cancel or even reverse the rectification due to the geometric asymmetry" ([Truccolo et al. 2024](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)). The Moddel-group PIC code reaches the same limits from the other side: with zero field the asymmetry is exactly 1, and as λ_MFP → 0 "the asymmetry approaches 1" ([Stearns & Moddel 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)).

The consequence for the simulator proposed in the brief is therefore not a matter of taste. **A purely field-free event-driven ray tracer—straight-line flights, hard walls, any specularity, no scattering, no potential—is structurally unable to produce any I–V asymmetry for any geometry, Tesla or otherwise.** It is nevertheless valuable, as a geometry, contact-injection and detailed-balance validation engine (its *required* output is A = 1 for every shape, which is a strong test). The minimum rectification-capable model needs (i) an in-device potential, at least an applied uniform field, preferably Poisson-coupled, and (ii) a momentum-relaxation model, because the asymmetry magnitude is governed by λ_MFP relative to the funnel length and vanishes in both the λ → 0 and (in the Moddel model) the λ → ∞ limits. Once a field is present the analytic straight-line free flight is partly lost: for massless Dirac carriers the constant-field flight is not even a parabola (speed is fixed at v_F while the direction rotates), and Truccolo's closed form involves a square-root-plus-logarithm expression whose intersection with a wall segment requires a nonlinear root find ("bisection") rather than a linear ray–segment test; they show the analytic flight at Δt = 10⁻¹⁶ s matches a linear stepping scheme only when the latter uses 10⁻¹⁷ s ([Truccolo thesis](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)). Field-free analytic flights should survive only as a validation mode.

## Every rectifying Tesla valve is hydrodynamic, so the loops buy resistance, not asymmetry

The three verified solid-state Tesla valves are consistent with the theorem above and with each other. Geurs et al. built an hBN-encapsulated single-loop graphene valve with a graphite back gate and found that "Rectification occurs at carrier densities and temperatures consistent with the hydrodynamic regime, and disappears both in the ballistic and diffusive transport regimes"; the viscous signal is a region with **D ≈ 1.05 for 20 K < T < 100 K at hole densities |n| > 5·10¹¹ cm⁻²**, with the stated regime condition l_ee ≪ W ≪ l_imp, l_ph; at 2 K (ballistic) there is no viscous rectification and at 110 K "the device acts as an Ohmic resistor" ([Geurs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04862)). The same paper identifies a trivial, regime-independent background that every gated two-terminal graphene device has: near charge neutrality the local carrier density depends on the sign and magnitude of the current, "reminiscent of the spatially varying carrier density in the channel of a field-effect transistor", which "causes unintended rectification irrespective of temperature and the transport regime" and scales inversely with gate voltage ([Geurs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04862)). Any measured or simulated Tesla asymmetry must have this subtracted before being attributed to geometry. The GaAs valve (n = 6.8×10¹¹ cm⁻², µ ≈ 10⁶ cm²/Vs, 2.5–5 µm channels, 4–70 K) shows forward and reverse I–V that "coincide" below ≈300 µA and diverge sharply above, with diodicity "as high as Di ≈ 40 in wider valve"; the authors explicitly argue that micron-scale widths support hydrodynamics "while excluding ballistic mechanisms", estimate Re ≳ 7, and describe the graphene result as "a vanishing rectification of ∼5 %" ([Sarypov et al. 2026, arXiv v2, not peer reviewed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16443)). The graphite phonon valve gives a 15.2 % conductivity difference at 45 K, D ≈ 1 in silicon valves of the same geometry and in the diffusive regime, and in the ballistic regime (≈10 K) forward/reverse conductivities of 4.18 vs 4.14 W m⁻¹K⁻¹, because "ballistic phonons excited by the heat source in random directions can traverse the entire structure"; the authors conclude that "hydrodynamic phonon behaviour is required to observe thermal rectification in a Tesla valve" ([Huang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11525177)). No magnon Tesla valve was found, and the only "quantum Tesla valve" claim is a 2017 conference abstract with no method whose extinction ratio >50 cannot be a stationary two-terminal transmission ratio ([indico record, abstract only](https://indico.global/event/8304/contributions/76244/)).

The judgment that follows is the one the brief asks to make explicit. **The Tesla geometry has no demonstrated ballistic mechanism beyond what any asymmetric shape offers.** Its mechanism in fluids and in the electron fluid is the inertial (v·∇)v term and viscous dissipation at the bifurcation/recombination, both of which are collective nonlinearities absent for independent particles—Geurs: "Momentum is then conserved for individual electrons but no collective response can occur." In the ballistic regime, a Tesla channel is just an asymmetric hard-wall billiard with a field inside it, i.e. a geometric diode whose G₂ is set by the same Christen–Büttiker physics as a funnel's, with two penalties. The loops roughly double or triple the path length per stage, adding series resistance in both directions and lowering λ_MFP/L_d, which Truccolo et al. 2026 identify as the controlling parameter: ballistic asymmetry is "preserved for MFP/Ld ≥ 1, while it declines for MFP/Ld < 1, approaching unity at MFP/Ld < 10⁻²", and "achieving proper diode functionality requires MFP/Ld > 0.1" ([Truccolo et al. 2026](https://iris.unimore.it//retrieve/handle/11380/1403348/971969/TruccoloTED2026.pdf)). And the loop channels of 75–150 nm width are exactly the regime where edge scattering collapses the apparent mean free path to a few times the feature size (next-but-one section). The honest framing is therefore **"Tesla-inspired shapes as one family within a general geometric-diode optimizer"**, with success judged against funnels, inverse arrowheads and Z-diodes at equal neck width, footprint and series resistance.

A distinct option is to take the Tesla geometry where it has a real mechanism. In hBN-encapsulated graphene the electron–electron length l_ee = ħv_F ε_F/(k_B T)² falls from ≈10 µm at 20 K to ≲0.2 µm at the fluidity onset around 100–150 K, while the electron–phonon length at 100 K and 10¹² cm⁻² is ≈6 µm, so micron-scale channels at 20–100 K satisfy l_ee ≪ W ≪ l_mr ([Bandurin et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6208423/)). The governing equations are Navier–Stokes-like with viscosity η = ¼v_F l_ee n m and a slip boundary condition whose length is ζ ≈ 0.6 l_ee for diffuse edges and parametrically larger for specular ones ([Kiselev & Schmalian 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.03933)); the Gurzhi (viscous) conductance of a constriction, G_vis = πn²e²w²/(32η), adds to the Sharvin value ([Guo et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.07269)) and has been seen experimentally as "superballistic" conductance below ≈150 K ([Krishna Kumar et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06672)). This is a finite-element problem of a kind the user already solves, and no electron-Navier–Stokes simulation of a Tesla geometry exists in the literature (Geurs show only a COMSOL fluid model; a 2022 unipolar hydrodynamic model of a 10 nm-scale graphene Tesla valve predicted D = 1.0655 with only qualitative comparison to experiment ([Materials 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9228317/))). The demonstrated diodicity, however, is small (≈1.05 in graphene), and the GaAs Di ≈ 40 is a threshold phenomenon above 300 µA at ≤20 K in a single unreviewed preprint, so this pivot is a physics-exploration project rather than a device project.

### The one "graphene ballistic Tesla valve" in print is a four-terminal chevron rectifier bridge

The Sejong/Manchester group that made the 2021 benchmark funnels published "Thermal noise rectification with graphene" in 2025 ([Nguyen et al., Nano Energy 136, 110687](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nanoen.2025.110687), CC-BY; read in full from the publisher PDF, supplementary not available). Its abstract calls the device "a graphene ballistic diode based on the Tesla valve structure", and the paper is therefore the closest thing to prior art for the present proposal. Read in full, it does not support a ballistic Tesla-valve mechanism, for four reasons. First, the Tesla valve appears only as a sketch of the "design idea" (their Fig. 2b); the fabricated device in the SEM image (Fig. 2c) is a chain of nested chevron/arrowhead graphene elements pointing the same way with a neck width of ≈150 nm, i.e. the same funnel/arrowhead family as their 2021 devices and their 2022 "ballistic diode bridge" ([Nguyen et al. 2022, ACS Appl. Electron. Mater. 4, 1518](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsaelm.1c01035)), with no loop channels or re-entrant junctions. Second, the measurement is four-terminal: a current I_in is driven between one pair of contacts and V_out is read across a second pair (circuit insets of their Fig. 3), and V_out is *even* in I_in ("parabolic" for holes, "V-shaped" for electrons, with V_out reaching ≈25–45 mV at |I_in| = 50 µA). This is the Song-type ballistic-rectifier/bridge response that reciprocity allows at second order; the paper reports no two-terminal I(+V)/I(−V) asymmetry at all. Third, the physics invoked is the standard geometric-diode picture (specular edge reflection, λ_MFP versus neck width, gate-tunable carrier type that reverses the output sign, V_out = 0 at the Dirac point, 3–6× larger output at cryogenic temperature), and the improved DC efficiency (up to ≈10 % versus 2.6–10 % across four of twelve devices) is attributed by the authors to graphene quality, lithography resolution and "increasing the number of diodes on each channel", not to Tesla geometry; no same-neck-width non-Tesla control is reported. Fourth, the headline thermal-noise results (≈12.5 µV for electrons with a 30 kΩ source resistor, saturating above 30 kΩ, and ≈25 µV with *no* source connected, attributed to the device rectifying its own Johnson noise) are claims about rectifying an equilibrium noise source, which the second law forbids for a device in thermal equilibrium with that source (the Brillouin paradox); the paper does not address this, so those numbers should be treated as unexplained offsets or non-equilibrium pickup rather than as a diode figure of merit. The paper itself cites the graphite phonon Tesla valve ([Huang et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11525177)) as the model for its temperature dependence and correctly notes that in that system ballistic transport gives "a less pronounced rectification effect" while hydrodynamic flow enhances it, which is the opposite of what a ballistic Tesla valve would need. Net effect on this review: the only published "graphene ballistic Tesla valve" is a chevron rectifier bridge measured in a four-terminal configuration; it leaves the two-terminal verdict untouched and, if anything, confirms that the field's working mechanism is the gated, drifted-distribution geometric-diode effect at ≈150 nm necks.

## Figures of merit: A ≈ 1 + 2(G₂/G₁)V, and graphene geometric diodes have never exceeded 2

Because every two-terminal metric is an expression of G₂/G₁, the field's numbers can be put on one scale. With I'(0) = G₁ and I''(0) = 2G₂, the zero-bias current responsivity β = ½I''/I' = G₂/G₁ (A/W), the curvature coefficient γ = I''/I' = 2G₂/G₁ (V⁻¹), and the asymmetry ratio to leading order A(V) ≈ 1 + 2(G₂/G₁)V. The CVD-graphene diode with A = 1.40 at 2 V and β = 0.0628 A/W is internally consistent with G₂/G₁ ≈ 0.1 V⁻¹ ([Wang et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399968/)). Realistic graphene values—A ≈ 1.1–2.0 at 0.2–2 V, β ≲ 0.6 A/W, γ ≲ 1 V⁻¹—sit roughly two orders of magnitude below the ≈33–40 V⁻¹ curvature of tunnel or Schottky diodes; the geometric diode's selling point in the rectenna literature is its attofarad capacitance (RC ≈ 10⁻¹⁵ s), not its nonlinearity ([Zhu et al. 2013, abstract](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0022-3727/46/18/185101)). Four-terminal ballistic rectifiers are a different device class: their output V_LU ∝ I_SD² is transverse and even in current, so they need no two-terminal asymmetry and do not bear on the Tesla question; the Manchester graphene rectifier reaches 23,000 mV/mW and NEP 0.64 pW/Hz^½ ([Auton et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)), and the Brownless/Zhang/Song Carbon 2020 paper extends the Büttiker–Landauer theory of exactly these four-terminal devices to coexisting electrons and holes, with the ">650 GHz" statement referring to the group's 0.685 THz imaging demonstration ([Manchester record, abstract only](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/graphene-ballistic-rectifiers-theory-and-geometry-dependence)).

The review of the five named papers reduces to this. **Stearns & Moddel 2021** is a MATLAB Drude PIC code: macroparticles start at random positions with random v_F directions (no Fermi–Dirac reservoirs, no cosine flux weighting, no Pauli blocking), effective-mass Newtonian dynamics with m* = 0.02, exponential collision times τ = λ/v_F with isotropic reset, perfectly specular walls, and a 2D in-plane Poisson solve with a uniform neutralising background rather than a gate/oxide image charge; its only validation is a Drude-conductance match on a 10 × 2.5 µm strip and it gives no numeric asymmetries in the text ([Stearns & Moddel 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)). Its non-monotonic A(λ) is model-specific and depends on particles persisting and multi-bouncing indefinitely in a closed box. **Truccolo et al. 2024** and the **Truccolo thesis** are the opposite lineage and the only one with a verification ladder: analytic Dirac free flights inside mesh triangles, Fermi–Dirac energy sampling with ½cos β angular injection and statistical weights, NGP charge deposition, frozen-field MC (10⁵ steps of ≈0.1 fs) alternating with a Newton–Raphson nonlinear 3D Poisson solve to 10⁻⁷ V on ≈10⁶ unknowns, verified against COMSOL (≈3 mV discrepancy at the neck), an analytic capacitor (n = 9.972×10¹⁵ m⁻² vs 9.97×10¹⁵ simulated), the finite-temperature ballistic conductance formula, and the solenoidal condition I_DS = −I_SD ([Truccolo thesis](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)). The 2026 follow-up adds e/h transport, LA/LO/remote-phonon and random-potential scattering, and the MFP/L_d collapse, and is the only semiclassical model quantitatively fitted to a fabricated device ([Truccolo et al. 2026](https://iris.unimore.it//retrieve/handle/11380/1403348/971969/TruccoloTED2026.pdf)). **Brownless/Zhang/Song 2020** is four-terminal theory and does not bear on two-terminal asymmetry; its full text was inaccessible, so the four fabricated designs and fitted parameters are unverified. **Wang et al. 2024** holds the record A = 1.97 but is abstract-only: shoulder, length, temperature, bias, density and the exact asymmetry formula are unknown, and the "three states" very likely mean forward/zero/inverted output for electrons/CNP/holes, as Nguyen's verified data show (γ > 1 for V_g > V_D, γ < 1 below) ([Nguyen et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41699-021-00269-2)).

For a fair benchmark the recommendation is a three-tier set. The primary experimental target is **Nguyen 2021 Device D (and B)**: complete dimensions, λ, lever arm, temperature, pulsed four-quadrant protocol, and γ versus both I and V_g. Two cautions apply: γ is a voltage asymmetry at fixed current, not A at fixed voltage, so the simulator must evaluate the same quantity; and the device is in the field-driven regime (linear within ±20 µA, nonlinear above, ≈1 V across 6–10 µm), so a linear-response or field-free model predicts γ → 1 by construction. The null benchmark is the **Lemme/Truccolo 2026 CVD device**, the only diode with every geometric parameter numeric, which tests the scattering model (a correct code must *not* produce the 1.20 ballistic asymmetry when MFP/L_d ≈ 10⁻²). The code-to-code benchmark is **Truccolo 2024**: T_DS = T_SD = 0.1 at zero field, A ≈ 1.05–1.15 at low uniform-field bias, halving with 3D electrostatics, A < 1 at α_d = 7.6°, plus the 2026 scaling result A = 1.22–1.24 for ballistic X1–X4. The record device cannot be a benchmark until its full text (or the H. Wang KAUST thesis) is read, and even then its neck is quantum.

The expected model gap must be built into tolerances. Nguyen et al. note that "theory predicts a larger rectification ratio as compared to the experiment": Stearns & Moddel ≈5 for a Device-A-like geometry (measured 1.22), El-Araby ≈10, Berdiyorov ≈5, while "the experimental values for the current rectification do not exceed 2" ([Nguyen et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41699-021-00269-2)); the 2024 KAUST abstract-level summary says the same of its own specular, non-interacting simulations ([search snippet, not read](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aelm.202300695)). Specular-wall, uniform-field models therefore overshoot by roughly **2–4× in A**, and a new code that lands inside that band with specular walls is behaving like the literature, not failing.

## Classical trajectories screen and diagnose; they do not predict the asymmetry

Can an event-driven classical trajectory code predict the target metric? For the device class and operating points that matter, the answer is that it can **bound and rank** but not **predict**, for four separable reasons, each with a documented magnitude. First, the field-free billiard gives A = 1 identically (previous section), so prediction requires the in-device potential, and the uniform-field shortcut overestimates A by about 2× relative to self-consistent electrostatics and misses a sign-reversal regime ([Truccolo et al. 2024](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)). Second, the edge model is the largest unknown: no graphene paper reports a fitted Fuchs specularity, the proxies disagree (focusing-peak ratios of 0.2–0.5 for O₂-plasma edges ([Taychatanapat et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.1969)) versus "nearly zero specularity" from boundary magnetoresistance ([Masubuchi et al. 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5150)) and fully diffuse cosine-law edges needed to fit scanning-gate images of RIE-etched devices ([Bhandari et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02248))), and theory shows disordered edge modes make reflection diffusive even when λ_F ≫ the roughness correlation length ([Walter et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1804.04142)). Every published geometric-diode MC assumes p = 1, the less physical limit, and no quantitative specular-vs-diffuse asymmetry comparison exists anywhere in the literature—so p ∈ [0, 0.5] with p ≈ 0.2–0.3 central must be swept, and a Soffer-type angle dependence p(θ) matters for the many grazing hits in long straight Tesla walls. Third, independent-particle MC cannot capture e–e scattering: at 300 K l_ee is of order 0.1–0.3 µm (extrapolated from the T⁻² anchors, researcher's estimate), comparable to or shorter than l_mr, and in graphene pairwise collisions do not conserve ensemble velocity because of the linear dispersion, so a naive e–e operator is wrong; a code without a momentum-conserving two-body operator will never show the superballistic or viscous effects and should give G ≤ G_ball always ([Guo et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.07269)). Fourth, and decisive for the record device, **necks of 23–26 nm are quantum**: λ_F = 2π/√(πn) is 35 nm at 10¹² cm⁻² and 25 nm at 2×10¹², so a 25 nm neck carries N = k_F W/π ≈ 1.4 modes at 10¹², and the mode spacing ΔE = πħv_F/W ≈ 83 meV exceeds k_BT at 300 K (26 meV), so quantization is not thermally washed out even at room temperature (computed values; formula sources [Guo et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1607.07269), [Banszerus et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08601)). Classical ray counting predicts G ∝ W rather than a staircase and ignores evanescent transmission; large-scale tight-binding/NEGF reproduces semiclassical caustic positions but shows diffraction has "major implications" for apertures smaller than λ_F ([Calogero et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.10220)). Auton's 50 K oscillations with 15.1 meV spacing in 120–140 nm leads are an experimental instance the billiard "does not explain" ([Auton et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)). **A Landauer–Büttiker/tight-binding check (Kwant or TBtrans) is therefore required for any feature below ≈50 nm**, and no published Kwant study of a graphene geometric diode or Tesla valve exists to lean on.

Where classical trajectories demonstrably work is also clear: feature sizes ≳ 100 nm, fields with r_c ≫ λ_F, temperatures where coherence is lost (interference fine structure gone by ≈15–60 K) but l_mr still exceeds the path, and angle- or position-integrated observables—cyclotron-orbit images, focusing peak positions, Veselago refraction, antidot commensurability ([Bhandari et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.02248); [Chen et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1602.08182)). For the Tesla question this means an event-driven code is the right tool for ranking shape families at fixed physics, mapping the sensitivity of A to p, λ, R_c and corner radius, visualising where trajectories are lost in loops, and computing transmission matrices to feed a Landauer–Büttiker assembly (as Truccolo's four-terminal chapter does). It is not the tool that will tell the user whether a 25 nm-neck device reaches 1.97 or 2.5; that number lives in the quantum neck, the self-consistent potential and the edge disorder, three things a classical ray tracer approximates at best.

## Fabrication realism: a Tesla stage is quasi-ballistic at best, and the neck may not exist electrically

The fabrication record for encapsulated graphene sets the geometric envelope. The demonstrated minimum neck is 23 nm ([Wang et al. 2024, abstract](https://doaj.org/article/4bd10bb4571448c382f1b1e72a72dbea)); standard processing is PMMA with CHF₃/O₂ or SF₆ RIE, and the in-plane graphene opening is smaller than the top-hBN opening because of the conical etch profile—40 nm lithographic antidots came out 25–30 nm wide ([Sandner et al. 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207)). Cryo-etching (Ar/SF₆ at −110 °C) is the one encapsulated process with measured edge roughness, ≈2 nm AFM on a 206 nm constriction ([Clericò et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07459)), whereas conventional hard-mask RIE needed a 13 nm roughness amplitude with 5 nm correlation length to fit a 230 nm constriction's conductance (c₀ ≈ 0.56), attributed to microcracks ([Terrés et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844)). Below ≈50 nm, features print round ("most features below 50 nm ... have no sharp corners" ([EIPBN 2009](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2009/papers/P-1F-14.pdf))), so every re-entrant Tesla junction will be blunted by a corner radius of order 5–25 nm; no measured corner radius or chip-level CD distribution exists for graphene. The RWTH review states that for etched constrictions "roughly < 200 nm" rough edges play "a dominant and unfortunately limiting role", with localized states and Coulomb blockade "making the mean free path ... nearly meaningless" ([Somanchi et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09781)), and a 20 nm bilayer constriction made without RIE became a single quantum dot with >100 meV addition energy ([Rienstra et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08758)).

The electrically active width is the single largest uncertainty for a Tesla neck. The literature is bimodal: Terrés et al. conclude that "reflections at the rough edges ... and not a reduction in active channel width" explain their data, and Sandner et al. attribute the shrinkage to the etch profile with "a lack of a depletion region in graphene"; yet Auton et al. infer a 71 nm electrical width for 122–137 nm AFM leads, i.e. 25–33 nm per side, "owing to edge states and traps" ([Auton et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)). A 25 nm CAD neck can therefore have an electrical width anywhere from ≈25 nm to pinched off, and the model must be run as a distribution over dead-strip width (0–20 nm per side), CD bias (−5 to −8 nm per side for 40 nm openings), corner radius (5–25 nm), LER (1–3 nm RMS for cryo-etch, 5–15 nm for conventional RIE) and device-to-device CD spread (≈±5 nm, weakly sourced). Operating at n ≥ 2×10¹² cm⁻² (λ_F ≈ 25 nm) is needed to keep ≥2 modes open and stay outside the edge-state window, which for a 230 nm channel already spans |n| < 0.45–0.75×10¹² cm⁻² and widens after air exposure ([Terrés et al. 2016](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844)).

Contact resistance dilutes whatever asymmetry survives. Best-case 1D edge contacts are ≈100–118 Ω·µm ([Wang et al. 2013](https://bioee.ee.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Science-2013-Wang-614-7.pdf)), but measured two-terminal nanodevices show 0.4–3.2 kΩ total: 0.8–1.4 kΩ in Auton's rectifier, 410–518 Ω in 100 nm constrictions ([Caridad et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5811439/)), 3.2 kΩ for an 85 nm SiO₂ constriction. A 25 nm neck has a Landauer resistance of 6.5 kΩ at one mode and ≈2.0 kΩ at 5×10¹² cm⁻² (computed), so with an intrinsic asymmetry of 2.0 the measured value is (2R + R_c)/(R + R_c): for R = 2 kΩ, R_c = 1 kΩ gives 1.67 and R_c = 2.8 kΩ gives 1.42 (computed). The simulator must carry R_c as an explicit, density-dependent series element diverging at the CNP, and report intrinsic and two-terminal figures separately. Multi-stage Tesla devices in series raise the neck:contact ratio but multiply the resistance and cut the current.

Does a Tesla-like geometry retain useful ballistic behaviour after all this? With 25 nm necks, 75–150 nm main channels and loop radii at least equal to the channel width, a stage is ≈300–600 nm long and 3–5 stages span ≈1–3 µm (geometric assumption; no graphene Tesla or loop channel at sub-100 nm width has been published). Against bulk mean free paths of ≈0.5–1.5 µm at room temperature (µ ≈ 1–2×10⁵ cm²/Vs; [Mayorov et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4510); [Banszerus et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08601)) and 15–28 µm at ≤4 K, the device-level λ/L is ≈0.3–1 at RT and ≈7–20 at 4 K. But nanopatterning collapses the *apparent* mean free path toward the feature scale: a 100 nm-period antidot lattice in encapsulated graphene showed an apparent l of 160 nm against an intrinsic ≥400 nm ([Sandner et al. 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207)). A Matthiessen-type 1/l_eff = 1/l_bulk + 1/l_edge with l_edge ≈ W/(1−p) reproduces this for p ≈ 0.3–0.5, so in 50–150 nm Tesla channels l_eff is plausibly only 100–300 nm *at any temperature*, cryogenic bulk mean free paths buy little, and the per-stage transport is quasi-ballistic at room temperature (MFP/L_stage ≈ 0.2–1) and edge-limited ballistic only at cryogenic temperatures. By Truccolo's criterion (MFP/L_d > 0.1 for functional diode action, ≥1 for the full ballistic asymmetry), a multi-stage Tesla device is on the edge of functionality at RT, which is the same conclusion reached from the mechanism side: the geometry's extra length is a cost paid in both directions.

## Staged simulator plan: six gates, no CUDA until Stage 2 physics is believed

The plan is deliberately narrow at the front. Each stage ends in a decision gate; a failed gate stops the programme or redirects it rather than being patched around. Implementation detail is kept at the level needed to act.

**Stage 0 — field-free validation engine (C++, serial/OpenMP, 2D).** Polyline/arc geometry with per-segment boundary type (reflecting with specularity p and optional Soffer p(θ), absorbing contact), straight-line analytic free flights with exact segment intersection, Fermi-surface carriers at k_F with ½cos θ cosine injection uniformly along the contact, Philox-style counter RNG keyed by particle index from the start (so Stage 3 results can be checked bit-for-bit against this code), transmission-matrix and two-reservoir current estimators, trajectory dumps for diagnostics. Deliverables are tests T1–T6. *Gate 0:* all six pass, and the Tesla family returns A = 1 within statistics; the team records the Stage-0 transmission matrices of candidate Tesla shapes versus funnels as the "series-resistance cost" baseline (how much T falls in both directions when loops are added). This stage can never show rectification, and that is its point.

**Stage 1 — applied field, momentum relaxation, Fermi–Dirac injection, bias-window estimator.** Replace straight flights by constant-field Dirac flights (closed form per Truccolo, with bisection on the wall intersection, or a cheap adaptive integrator—benchmark both against the 10⁻¹⁶ vs 10⁻¹⁷ s test), add a Poisson-process momentum reset with 1/τ = v_F/l and isotropic final direction (the Jacoboni–Reggiani free-flight/self-scattering limit ([Jacoboni & Reggiani 1983](https://www.doi.org/10.1103/REVMODPHYS.55.645))), inject energies from p(E) ∝ E f(E − µ) (Dirac DOS × Fermi–Dirac, as in the Udine code), and implement the bias-window estimator (weight f_S − f_D, every particle contributes to net current, zero identically at V = 0) alongside the two-reservoir one. Uniform field E = V/L is the first potential model, with a clearly labelled "non-self-consistent" flag. Deliverables are T7 and T8, and the Stearns–Moddel vs Udine discriminating experiment: does A(λ) at fixed geometry plateau (Udine, reservoirs and absorbing contacts) or peak at λ ≈ 6 W_n and fall (Moddel, closed box)? A reservoir code should plateau; this is a known open disagreement a new code can settle. *Gate 1:* T7–T8 pass; the Tesla family is compared with funnel/Z/inverse-arrowhead at equal W_n, footprint and series resistance under identical uniform field and λ. If no Tesla candidate matches the best conventional shape's A at ≤1.5× its resistance, Tesla shapes are demoted to one family in the optimizer and no Tesla-specific effort continues; if one does, it proceeds to Stage 2 as a candidate.

**Stage 2 — Poisson coupling and 3D dielectric stack.** Nonlinear Poisson with the graphene as a δ-sheet, Newton–Raphson on the exponential (Truccolo's Eq. 5 form, chosen because linear coupling excites plasma oscillations and demands Δt ≲ 1/(2ω_pl)), frozen-field MC between solves (the Udine ratio is 10⁵ MC steps of ≈0.1 fs per solve), NGP or CIC deposition, second-half time averaging, 3D domain of oxide + air/vacuum + back-gate Dirichlet with Neumann elsewhere. The user should build this on an unstructured FEM mesh rather than Truccolo's FDM to remove the staircasing error and handle curved Tesla walls natively; this is the one place the brief's over-engineering risk is justified, because the self-consistent potential is where the physics lives. Deliverables: capacitor and FEM cross-checks; reproduction of the self-consistent Truccolo trends (A halved vs uniform field, A < 1 at α_d = 7.6°, A decreasing with V_bk 3.38 → 30 V and with ε_r); the Geurs transistor-like background reproduced and subtractable; and T9 in full. *Gate 2:* T9 passes with p and R_c inside their sourced ranges; the Tesla candidate's self-consistent advantage (if any) survives. Only now is the physics believed enough to spend on performance.

**Stage 3 — CUDA.** One history per thread in a single megakernel (trace + physics), structure-of-arrays particle state, Philox4x32-10 with one subsequence per particle keyed by global index so output is independent of scheduling ([cuRAND documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/curand/host-api-overview.html)); XORWOW's seeded-ordering weaknesses and MRG32k3a's occasional marginal BigCrush results make them second choices. The lifetime tail is the dominant inefficiency for exactly this workload—a few particles trapped in long diffuse loops keep warps resident—so plan, in order of cost: a physical bounce/path cap (l_mr already supplies one), stream compaction/respawn every k events, persistent-thread queues, and Shader Execution Reordering on Ada/Hopper hardware, where an optical ray-tracing benchmark raised warp efficiency from 2.6/32 to 20.4/32 active lanes for a ≈15× wall-clock gain with bit-identical results ([SER study, 2026 preprint, single benchmark](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2608.21396)). The Poisson solve stays on the host or uses an existing GPU sparse solver; it is not the bottleneck at 10⁵ MC steps per solve. *Gate 3:* bitwise agreement with Stage 2 on T1–T9 (same seeds), ≥10× throughput on the Nguyen geometry, and the full p × λ × R_c × corner-radius sensitivity map for one Tesla and one funnel candidate in under a day.

**Stage 4 — quantum check for sub-50 nm features.** For any candidate with neck or junction features below ≈50 nm (which includes the record 23–26 nm device), compute T(E) with Kwant or TBtrans on the actual CAD-transformed outline with edge disorder (Terrés used ΔW = 0.2W; Walter et al.'s edge-mode resonance mechanism), compare the mode staircase N(E) with the classical aperture transmission, and feed the quantum neck transmission into the Landauer–Büttiker assembly with the Stage-2 self-consistent potential. *Gate 4:* if the quantum neck changes the predicted A by more than ≈20 % relative to the classical aperture, classical results for that geometry are reported only as trends, and the quantum model becomes the reference for that design point.

**Stage 5 — optimization and the hydrodynamic option.** Parametric shape families (funnel angle, Z-arms, inverse arrowhead, Tesla loop count/radius/neck), the fabrication transform as a distribution (per-edge bias −5 to −8 nm, corner radius 5–25 nm, LER 1–15 nm RMS, dead strip 0–20 nm, CD spread ±5 nm, series R_c 0.2–3 kΩ), objective A at fixed V or β = G₂/G₁, with resistance and current as constraints. Run in parallel, if the pivot is taken, a separate FEM electron-hydrodynamics solver (Navier–Stokes with η = ¼v_F l_ee n m, slip length ζ from Kiselev–Schmalian, 20–100 K, micron-scale Tesla loops), benchmarked against Geurs's D ≈ 1.05 window and the Gurzhi conductance formula. *Gate 5:* a design is declared worth fabricating only if its two-terminal A, after the fabrication distribution and R_c, exceeds the best conventional geometric diode at the same neck width by more than the model's demonstrated 2–4× uncertainty band—a deliberately hard bar.

## Go/no-go: no-go on the premise, conditional go on the reframed programme

The scientifically defensible verdict is a **no-go on the idea as stated**—a Tesla-valve-inspired two-terminal graphene channel functioning as a ballistic/geometric diode that outperforms funnels, Z-shapes and asymmetric rectifiers by virtue of its geometry—and a **conditional go on two reframings**. The no-go rests on three independent lines of evidence that all point the same way: the unitarity theorem that makes T_SD(E) = T_DS(E) for any shape, the Christen–Büttiker result that the only available even-order term is sourced by the bias-induced internal potential rather than wall geometry, and the empirical fact that all three verified solid-state Tesla valves rectify only in the hydrodynamic regime and show D = 1 within error in the ballistic limit. Against this, the Tesla geometry offers no new ballistic mechanism, costs series resistance in both directions, shortens λ_eff/L_d through edge scattering in its loops, and places its sharpest features where lithography rounds corners and where few-mode quantum transport invalidates the billiard picture. The first reframing—Tesla shapes as one family in a general geometric-diode optimizer with the staged, gated simulator above—is a sound engineering project whose realistic ceiling is the published geometric-diode range (A ≈ 1.1–2.0; β ≲ 0.6 A/W) and whose main scientific contribution would be the first quantitative specular-vs-diffuse edge study and the first reservoir-based resolution of the Moddel/Udine A(λ) disagreement. The second—electron hydrodynamics at 20–100 K—is where the Tesla geometry has an actual mechanism and the user's finite-element expertise is directly applicable, but its demonstrated payoff is D ≈ 1.05 in graphene and the GaAs Di ≈ 40 is a single unreviewed threshold result.

Four pieces of evidence would change this verdict. A measurement of a two-terminal Tesla-shaped graphene channel showing D ≠ 1 at ≤4 K and at high density (outside the Geurs hydrodynamic window, with the gate-induced transistor background subtracted) would demonstrate a ballistic mechanism the theory says cannot exist in linear response and would demand re-examination of the G₂ analysis for that geometry. The one paper that calls a graphene ballistic device a "Tesla valve" has now been read in full and does not qualify as such evidence: Nguyen et al., Nano Energy 136, 110687 (2025) ([DOI 10.1016/j.nanoen.2025.110687](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nanoen.2025.110687), CC-BY) is a four-terminal ballistic-rectifier bridge of nested chevron/arrowhead elements with ≈150 nm necks, not a looped two-terminal channel, and its output is even in the input current, which reciprocity permits (see the paragraph on this paper above). A Stage-4 Kwant calculation showing that a few-mode Tesla neck with a self-consistent potential yields a G₂ that beats a funnel of equal neck width would make the quantum regime the place to compete, with classical trajectories relegated to leads. And a Stage-1 result in which a Tesla candidate matches the best funnel's A at ≤1.5× its resistance under identical physics would justify carrying it through Stages 2–5 as a serious design, not merely a family member.

## Conclusion

The most useful thing this review changes is the question. "Can a Tesla valve rectify ballistic electrons?" has a theorem-grade answer—not by shape, only through the bias-induced potential, and then only at O(V²)—so the live questions become "which asymmetric shape, under self-consistent electrostatics, diffuse edges and real contacts, gives the largest G₂/G₁ per unit series resistance?" and "is the hydrodynamic window worth entering?" Both are answerable with the staged programme above, and both reward the user's actual strengths: the self-consistent Poisson problem on curved outlines is a finite-element problem, the electron-hydrodynamic pivot is literally Navier–Stokes with a slip boundary condition, and the acceptance-test discipline of T1–T9 is the kind of verification ladder the finite-element community already expects. The one trap to avoid is the one the published literature has repeatedly fallen into: a specular-wall, imposed-field particle code that "shows rectification" and is taken as prediction rather than as the 2–4× upper bound it has always turned out to be.

A final implication concerns where the real uncertainty lives. Across every source, the quantities the Tesla design is most sensitive to—edge specularity, electrically dead edge width, corner radius, and the few-mode transmission of a 25 nm neck—are precisely the quantities nobody has measured for etched encapsulated graphene. A simulator that treats them as swept distributions and reports asymmetry as a distribution, not a number, will be more honest than any published geometric-diode model; a simulator that reports a single A for a single CAD outline will be reproducing the field's central error with better hardware.
