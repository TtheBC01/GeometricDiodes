# Experimentally demonstrated graphene geometric diodes and ballistic rectifiers: benchmark catalogue

Scope note: "Verified" below means I read the statement in the paper's own text (open-access full text, PMC copy, or the publisher abstract page). "Secondhand" means the number comes from a later paper's summary of the original (mainly Truccolo et al. IEEE TED 2024/2026, Nguyen et al. 2021), because the original (Zhu 2013 J. Phys. D, Moddel 2012 SSC, Wang 2024 Adv. Electron. Mater., Brownless 2020 Carbon, Auton 2017 Nano Lett.) is paywalled or blocked to automated fetching. Secondhand values are flagged in-line.

Two device classes are kept separate throughout:
- **Two-terminal geometric diodes (GD)**: funnel/arrowhead or "inverse arrowhead" constriction between two contacts; figure of merit is the I–V asymmetry A (or γ) at a given |V| or |I|.
- **Four-terminal ballistic rectifiers (BR, "Song-type")**: asymmetric cross junction (often with a triangular antidot); input is an a.c. current between source S and drain D, output is a d.c. transverse voltage between upper U and lower L leads; figure of merit is responsivity V_out,dc / P_in (V/W or mV/mW) and NEP. The output is even in the input current, so no two-terminal I–V asymmetry (and no violation of two-terminal reciprocity) is required.

## Key Question 1 — Brownless/Zhang/Song, "Graphene ballistic rectifiers: Theory and geometry dependence", Carbon 2020: authors, geometries, theory, the ">650 GHz" statement, and whether these are four-terminal BRs

### Takeaway
The paper is Joseph Brownless, Jiawei Zhang and Aimin Song, Carbon 168, 201–208 (2020), DOI 10.1016/j.carbon.2020.06.058. It extends the Büttiker–Landauer-based analytical theory of the four-terminal Song-type ballistic rectifier to graphene's coexisting electrons and holes, fits four fabricated asymmetric BR designs, and predicts ≥50,800 V/W and 0.51 pW/Hz^1/2; the abstract's "previously shown to operate beyond 650 GHz" refers to the group's earlier antenna-coupled graphene BR THz detector (Auton et al., Nano Lett. 2017; imaging at 0.685 THz). These are four-terminal rectifiers, not two-terminal diodes.

### Cited Findings

- Abstract content (Manchester explorer / Semantic Scholar summaries): graphene BRs exploit high mobility to rectify ballistic carriers in an asymmetric nanostructure without a bandgap; "the device has been previously shown to operate beyond 650 GHz"; the authors "extend the previously developed Büttiker–Landauer formula to an analytical theory that handles electrons and holes coexisting in semimetal graphene"; four rectifier designs were fabricated and compared, the theory fits their characteristics and "the derived parameters agree well with the device geometries"; predicted responsivities ≥ ~50,800 V/W and NEP ~0.51 pW/Hz^1/2 (predictions, not measurements); responsivity increases with larger difference between electron and hole mobilities — [Manchester Research Explorer](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/graphene-ballistic-rectifiers-theory-and-geometry-dependence); [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.carbon.2020.06.058?fields=title,abstract,openAccessPdf,tldr)

### Inferences
- The "beyond 650 GHz" claim in the Carbon abstract almost certainly refers to the 0.685 THz imaging demonstration of Auton et al. Nano Lett. 2017 (same group; 685 GHz > 650 GHz); the measured flat response was only verified to 0.44–0.45 THz, with 0.685 THz being a single-frequency imaging demonstration, so "operation beyond 650 GHz" means detectable rectified output, not a characterised roll-off.
- Because the Carbon 2020 devices are four-terminal BRs, their rectification is a transverse (Hall-like) even-in-current response governed by multi-terminal Büttiker–Landauer transmission asymmetries; this does not bear on whether a two-terminal graphene constriction can show an asymmetric I–V. For the two-terminal case the relevant nonlinear formalism is Christen–Büttiker (1996) / Song PRB (1999), as the Nguyen paper's own citation chain indicates.

## Key Question 2 — "A Graphene Geometric Diode with the Highest Asymmetry Ratio and Three States Gate-Tunable Rectification Ability" (Wang et al.): journal, numbers, stack, definition, three-state meaning

### Takeaway
The paper is Heng Wang, Maolin Chen, Yiming Yang, Yinchang Ma, Linqu Luo, Chen Liu, Igor Getmanov, Thomas D. Anthopoulos, Xixiang Zhang, Atif Shamim (KAUST), Advanced Electronic Materials 10(4), 2300695 (2024; online 28 Dec 2023), DOI 10.1002/aelm.202300695. It reports h-BN/monolayer-graphene/h-BN two-terminal geometric diodes with 23 nm and 26 nm necks, best asymmetry ratio 1.97, zero-bias current responsivity 0.6 A/W, voltage responsivity 12,000 V/W, and a rectified DC output that can be modulated and inverted by gate voltage. Dimensions beyond the neck, temperature, bias, carrier density and mean free path are not in the abstract and the full text was inaccessible; a 2026 review gives secondhand mobility/MFP ranges.

### Cited Findings
- Bibliographic: authors H. Wang, M. L. Chen, Y. M. Yang, Y. C. Ma, L. Q. Luo, C. Liu, I. Getmanov, T. D. Anthopoulos, X. X. Zhang, A. Shamim; Advanced Electronic Materials, vol. 10, no. 4, art. 2300695; published 1 April 2024 (early view 28 Dec 2023); DOI 10.1002/aelm.202300695; keywords: asymmetry ratio, gate tunability, geometric diode, h-BN/monolayer graphene/h-BN heterojunction, responsivity — [Manchester Research Explorer record](https://research.manchester.ac.uk/en/publications/a-graphene-geometric-diode-with-the-highest-asymmetry-ratio-and-t/); [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/4bd10bb4571448c382f1b1e72a72dbea)

- Secondhand characterisation by Truccolo et al. 2026 (refs [5] = Nguyen 2021, [7] = Wang 2024): "only a few demonstrations of rectification have been reported in micrometer-scale devices under low bias conditions (IDS < 40 µA) [5], [7]. In these works, rectification was achieved through exceptional carrier mobilities, ranging between 1×10^5 and 1.8×10^5 cm2V−1s−1 and corresponding to mean free paths between 700 and 900 nm, respectively." — [Truccolo et al., IEEE TED 73(5), 2696 (2026), PDF](https://iris.unimore.it//retrieve/handle/11380/1403348/971969/TruccoloTED2026.pdf)
- The same review states the asymmetry definition used across the Moddel/KAUST lineage: "asymmetry A of the device current, defined as [1] and [2] A = I_DS(+V_DS)/I_DS(−V_DS)" — [Truccolo 2026 PDF](https://iris.unimore.it//retrieve/handle/11380/1403348/971969/TruccoloTED2026.pdf)
- Prior KAUST/Shamim device (Wang et al. 2021, CVD on SiO2) defined asymmetry as the magnitude of forward current divided by magnitude of backward current at a given V_DS, and responsivity as ½·I″(V)/I′(V) — [Wang et al., Nanomaterials 11, 1986 (2021), PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399968/)
- Precedent for polarity inversion with gate: Moddel, Zhu, Grover, Joshi, "Ultrahigh speed graphene diode with reversible polarity", Solid State Commun. 152(19), 1842–1845 (2012) — cited as ref [1] in [Truccolo 2024 PDF](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf) and as ref 24 (γ = 1.31) in [Nguyen 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41699-021-00269-2)
- Independent verified observation of carrier-type reversal in an hBN GD (Nguyen 2021): "If γ > 1, the electron transport is dominant, and the hole transport is dominant in case of γ < 1"; "γ is > 1 for Vg > VD and <1 for Vg < VD"; for device D "the forward direction of the device was markedly reversed by Vg" — [Nguyen 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41699-021-00269-2)
- Related KAUST PhD thesis: Heng Wang, "Design and Process Development of Graphene-Based Geometric Diodes for Enhanced Performance" (ECE, KAUST; defended 29 Apr 2025) — [KAUST CEMSE event listing](https://cemse.kaust.edu.sa/events/by-type/2025/04/29/design-and-process-development-graphene-based-geometric-diodes-enhanced)

### Inferences
- "Three states" very likely denotes: forward rectified output for one carrier type (e.g., n-type gate), ~zero output at the charge-neutrality point, and inverted output for the other carrier type — i.e., the rectification direction reverses with carrier sign, as the Moddel 2012 "reversible polarity" title and the verified Nguyen 2021 data (γ>1 electrons, γ<1 holes, γ≈1 at Dirac point) both show. This is consistent with the ratchet picture: holes and electrons are funnelled the same way geometrically but carry opposite current sign. Not verified from the Wang 2024 text itself.
- Given Nguyen 2021 reported 1.3×10^5 cm²/Vs and λ ≈ 0.7 µm, the Truccolo 2026 range implies Wang 2024 reported ≈1.8×10^5 cm²/Vs and MFP ≈ 900 nm, measured at room temperature and |I_DS| < 40 µA. Treat as secondhand.
- The 1.97 asymmetry ratio is most likely |I(+V)|/|I(−V)| at a fixed |V| (the Shamim/Moddel convention), but the bias at which 1.97 is reached is not in the abstract.
- A neck of 23–26 nm with MFP ≈ 0.9 µm gives MFP/W_neck ≈ 35–40, far into the ballistic regime for the constriction; the limiting factor becomes edge specularity and the channel length (micrometre scale per Truccolo 2026).

### Gaps
- Full text inaccessible (Wiley 403/bot challenge; no repository copy at KAUST found; CORE blocked). Not verified: shoulder/channel width, length, taper angle, hBN thicknesses, exfoliated vs CVD graphene, gate type (global Si back gate vs local), contacts (1D edge contacts likely, given citation of Wang 2013 Science), measurement temperature, bias range of the I–V, bias at which A = 1.97 occurs, carrier density vs V_G, MFP/mobility values, frequency of the rectification-circuit test, and the exact asymmetry-ratio formula.
- The reference-list entry "Rectifying Effect in a High-Performance Ballistic Diode Bridge Based on Encapsulated Graphene with a Unique Design" appears to be a further hBN-encapsulated graphene ballistic-diode experiment but could not be located by search.

## Key Question 3 — Auton et al. Nat. Commun. 7, 11670 (2016) graphene ballistic nano-rectifier; Song-group InGaAs BRs and THz operation (Balocco et al.)

### Takeaway
Auton et al. built a four-terminal asymmetric cross-junction BR in hBN-encapsulated exfoliated graphene (S lead 122 nm, D lead 137 nm wide by AFM; 1D edge contacts; 290 nm SiO2/Si back gate), with room-temperature mobilities 197,600 (e) / 106,000 (h) cm²/Vs, and measured an intrinsic responsivity of 23,000 mV/mW (V_out,dc / P_in,ac at 190 Hz, 1 µA input) and NEP 0.64 pW/Hz^1/2; frequency testing was limited to 3 MHz by parasitic RC of the conducting substrate. The 1.5 THz room-temperature result of Balocco et al. APL 2011 is for a self-switching diode (unipolar nanodiode), not a four-terminal BR; InGaAs BRs were demonstrated to 50 GHz (Song 2001).

### Cited Findings (all from the PMC full text unless noted)
- Device: "asymmetric four-terminal cross-junction (ballistic rectifier, BR) with source (S), drain (D), upper (U), and lower (L) terminals, patterned on a Hall bar"; narrowest source-lead width from AFM 122 nm, drain-lead width 137 nm; width inferred from lateral-quantization analysis W_SD = 71 nm (ΔE = ħv_F π/W_SD with peak spacing ΔE ≈ 15.1 meV), explained by "the ballistic region not extending fully to the edge, owing to edge states and traps" — [Auton 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Stack/fabrication: graphene exfoliated onto 290 nm SiO2, laminated between two hBN flakes (modified stamp transfer); e-beam lithography with bilayer PMMA (495K 100 nm / 950K 100 nm); CHF3/O2 RIE (5 W RF, 50 W ICP); 1D edge contacts, 3 nm Cr / 80 nm Au; contact resistances 0.8–1.4 kΩ; conducting Si substrate used as back gate — [Auton 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Transport: room-temperature field-effect mobilities 197,600 cm²/Vs (electrons) and 106,000 cm²/Vs (holes); Hall measurements of mobility and MFP vs density at 50, 200, 250 K (plotted, no numerical MFP in text); introduction: mobility above 100,000 cm²/Vs gives MFP "longer than 1 μm at room temperature"; neutrality point at V_G = −1 V; seven devices all >100,000 cm²/Vs with responsivity ~10,000 mV/mW — [Auton 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Metric: "The responsivity is determined by the output d.c. voltage divided by the input a.c. power"; optimum intrinsic responsivity 23,000 mV/mW measured at 190 Hz with low-amplitude 1 µA input; optimum gate ≈ +5 V (electrons) and ≈ −8 V (holes); R_LU ≈ 15 kΩ, R_SD ≈ 22 kΩ at zero gate; NEP 0.64 pW/Hz^1/2; measured noise at zero input 13.3 nV/Hz^1/2 vs thermal 15.7 nV/Hz^1/2; flicker corner as low as 2 kHz; noise sweep input 0.1–2.0 µA at 10 Hz — [Auton 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- SiO2 comparison: earlier SiO2-substrate graphene BR (Singh, Auton, Hill, Song, Carbon 84, 124–129 (2015), DOI 10.1016/j.carbon.2014.11.064) cited at 110 mV/mW in the introduction and 67 mV/mW in results; NEP of the hBN device "about three orders of magnitude better" — [Auton 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/); [Crossref record for Singh 2015](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.bibliographic=Graphene+ballistic+rectifiers+Theory+and+geometry+dependence+Carbon&rows=5)
- Temperature behaviour: output "in general increases as the temperature decreases"; quantum-like oscillations in dV_LU/dV_G at 150 K and 50 K; output reversal at low T for V_G > +13 V or < −12 V attributed to the Seebeck effect — [Auton 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Frequency: "The device could only be measured up to 3 MHz, limited by parasitic RC from the conducting substrate"; expectation of operation "well into the high THz frequency range"; suggests quartz substrate and local gate for high-frequency tests — [Auton 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Stability: measured in atmosphere over many weeks with no degradation, attributed to BN encapsulation — [Auton 2016, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Triangular BR variant: Auton, Kumar, Hill, Song, J. Electron. Mater. 46, 3942–3948 (2017): four-terminal device with triangular antidot, graphene mobility >100,000 cm²/Vs, responsivity 2400 mV/mW at room temperature with low-frequency input; low-T measurements used to justify ballistic behaviour — [Semantic Scholar record](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1007/s11664-016-4938-y?fields=title,abstract,year,venue,authors)
- Balocco et al., "Room-temperature operation of a unipolar nanodiode at terahertz frequencies", Appl. Phys. Lett. 98, 223501 (2011): room-temperature rectification at 1.5 THz from a unipolar nanodiode "based on symmetry breaking in a nanochannel" (self-switching diode), "the highest speed reported in nanorectifiers to date"; authors Balocco, Kasjoo, Lu, Zhang, Alimi, Winnerl, Song — [HZDR record Publ-16035](https://www.fzd.de/publications/Publ-16035)

- For a transport simulator, Auton 2016 is a good four-terminal benchmark (geometry partially specified, mobility/density/temperature/metric well specified), but it is not a two-terminal diode benchmark: the output is V_LU for a current I_SD and the relevant quantity is the second-order coefficient V_LU ≈ R_2 I_SD², not an I–V asymmetry.
- The user's "Balocco 2005/2011" THz references are self-switching-diode (two-terminal, field-effect) papers; the four-terminal InGaAs BR record remains 50 GHz (2001), with THz operation of BRs shown only in graphene (Auton 2017).

- Singh 2015 Carbon abstract not accessible (closed); the exact SiO2-device geometry and mobility are not verified.
- Balocco Nano Lett. 2005 ("Microwave detection at 110 GHz by nanowires with broken symmetry") was not found by search and is not verified.

## Key Question 4 — Moddel group geometric diodes (Zhu, Joshi, Grover, Moddel 2012–2014): device, metric, achieved asymmetry

### Takeaway
The Moddel group's exfoliated-graphene geometric diode (Solid State Commun. 2012; J. Phys. D 46, 185101 (2013); IEEE JSTQE 20, 2014) had neck ≈75 nm, shoulder 400 nm, MFP ≈45 nm (on SiO2), asymmetry A = I(+V)/I(−V) ≈ 1.3 (1.31) at V_DS = 1.5 V, zero-bias responsivity 0.0285 A/W, and rectified 10.6 µm (28 THz) radiation in an antenna-coupled rectenna; CVD-graphene versions gave A ≈ 1.1. All dimensional/asymmetry numbers are secondhand (Truccolo 2024, Nguyen 2021, Wang 2021); only the J. Phys. D abstract was read directly.

### Cited Findings
- Zhu, Joshi, Grover, Moddel, J. Phys. D: Appl. Phys. 46(18), 185101 (2013), received 30 Nov 2012, published 15 Apr 2013. Abstract (verified): "a new thin-film graphene diode called a geometric diode that relies on geometric asymmetry to provide rectification at 28 THz"; "planar structure of the geometric diode provides a low RC time constant, on the order of 10−15 s"; "Fabricated geometric diodes show asymmetric current–voltage characteristics consistent with Monte Carlo simulations"; rectennas with metal and graphene bowtie antennas "rectify 10.6 µm radiation, corresponding to an operating frequency of 28 THz" — [IOPscience abstract page](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0022-3727/46/18/185101)
- Secondhand dimensions/asymmetry: "authors manufactured a device with a neck aperture Wn ≃ 75 nm and a shoulder width Wc = 400 nm. The current asymmetry A ≃ 1.3 was observed for an applied voltage VDS = 1.5 V with exfoliated graphene on SiO2 substrate with MFP ≃ 45 nm. Asymmetry reduced to A ≃ 1.1 for CVD graphene with shorter MFP." (refs [1] Moddel 2012 SSC, [2] Zhu 2013 J. Phys. - Nguyen 2021 note on theory-vs-experiment gap: "Stearns and Moddel predicted ~5 with geometry similar to device A"; "El-Araby et al. predicted the asymmetry of ~10, and Berdiyorov et al. predicted ~5"; "the experimental values for the current rectification do not exceed 2" — [Nguyen 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41699-021-00269-2)

### Inferences
- With MFP ≈ 45 nm and neck 75 nm (MFP/W_neck ≈ 0.6) the 2013 device was only quasi-ballistic, which is consistent with the modest A ≈ 1.3 and the large bias (1.5 V) needed; it is a weaker benchmark than the hBN devices because the asymmetry is dominated by diffusive/field-driven effects that a billiard simulator will not capture without scattering.
- Moddel-group Monte Carlo models (Drude-model PIC) with specular walls over-predict asymmetry by ~2–4× relative to measurements, per Nguyen's comparison; any simulator benchmarked against these devices should expect to need partial diffuse edge scattering or heating to match.

## Key Question 5 — 2017–2026 geometric-diode experiments (CVD, hBN-encapsulated, other materials) and measured vs simulated asymmetry

### Takeaway
Verified experimental two-terminal graphene GD asymmetries to date cluster between 1.1 and 2.0: Zhu 2013 ≈1.3 (exfoliated/SiO2, 75 nm neck); Wang 2021 1.40 at ±2 V (CVD/SiO2, 50 nm neck, MFP 70 nm); Nguyen 2021 1.58–1.66 at 60–80 µA (hBN-encapsulated exfoliated, 125–175 nm neck, λ ≈ 0.7 µm, RT); Wang 2024 1.97 (hBN-encapsulated, 23–26 nm neck); Lemme/Truccolo 2026 CVD micrometre device: nearly linear (A barely above 1 at ±20 µA, fitted mobility ≈3000 cm²/Vs). ### Inferences
- Measured asymmetries in graphene GDs have never exceeded ~2.0 in any published experiment located here, whereas ballistic simulations with specular edges give 1.2 (for the modest Lemme geometry) up to 5–10 (optimised geometries). A simulator reproducing experiments must therefore include at least one of: partial diffuse edge scattering, substrate/remote-phonon scattering, self-consistent electrostatics (which Truccolo 2024 shows counteracts the geometric effect), bipolar transport near the CNP, and self-heating at high bias.
- The Nguyen 2021 geometry (neck 125–190 nm, channel 2 µm, length 6–10 µm, λ ≈ 0.7 µm) has MFP/neck ≈ 4–6 but MFP/L ≈ 0.1; its asymmetry grows with current because the field collimates carriers, i.e., it is not in the linear-response ballistic regime, and it needs ~1 V across the device. A Landauer linear-response simulator would predict γ → 1; a field-driven billiard/Monte Carlo at finite bias is needed.

- Carrier densities for the Nguyen benchmark points can be computed from the stated lever arm: n ≈ 7.2×10^10 × |V_g − V_D| cm⁻² → Device A at V_g = +2.5 V (V_D ≈ −2 V): ≈3.2×10^11 cm⁻²; Devices B/D at V_g ≈ +17–18 V: ≈1.4×10^12 cm⁻² (electrons); hole peak of device A at −5 V: ≈2×10^11 cm⁻². (My arithmetic from the paper's stated α and V_D; not stated in the paper.)

- The Truccolo 2026 Lemme device is the only experimental GD whose full geometry (W_n, W_d, L_d, L_c, α_d, t_ox) is numerically published, making it the most reproducible—but its asymmetry is near 1, so it tests a simulator's scattering model more than its ballistic geometry model.

## Key Question 7 — Typical mean free paths in hBN-encapsulated graphene (RT vs cryogenic) and edge-scattering statements

### Takeaway
In hBN-encapsulated exfoliated graphene, room-temperature MFP is ≈1–1.5 µm at n ≳ 2×10^11 cm⁻² (μ ≈ 100,000 cm²/Vs, limited by device width ≈1 µm in Mayorov 2011), rising to ≈3 µm below 50 K (μ ≈ 500,000); geometric-diode papers report λ ≈ 0.7 µm (μ = 1.3×10^5) to ≈0.9 µm (μ ≈ 1.8×10^5) at RT, versus 45–70 nm for graphene on SiO2. Every GD paper that discusses edges says real etched edges are partly diffusive and that this (plus bubbles/contamination and 'edge states and traps') lowers asymmetry below specular-wall predictions.

### Cited Findings
- Mayorov et al. 2011 (hBN/graphene/hBN, w ≈ 1 µm): SiO2-supported graphene "usually exhibits μ ~10,000 cm2V-1s-1"; for n ≈ 10^12 cm⁻² this gives MFP of order 100 nm; encapsulated devices "exhibit room-T ballistic transport over a 1 μm distance"; "At low n ~10^11 cm-2, the devices exhibit mobility μ >100,000 cm2V-1s-1 even at room T"; "at higher n ≈10^12 cm-2, we find that our devices' longitudinal conductivity σ becomes limited by their width w ≈1 μm rather than scattering in the bulk"; "encapsulated graphene can exhibit μ ~500,000 cm2V-1s-1 and l ≈3μm (at n ≈10^12 cm-2) at low T"; from bend resistance "li ≈1.5 μm at room T and ≈3 μm below 50 K for |n| >2x10^11 cm-2 (|Vg| >1 V)"; formula l = (h/2e)μ(n/π)^0.5; "l longer than 1 μm for all |Vg| >1V"; low-n μ across devices 20,000–150,000, "tending to ≈100,000 cm2V-1s-1 in most cases" — [Mayorov et al., arXiv:1103.4510 (Nano Lett. 11, 2396 (2011))](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.4510)
- GD-literature statements: "graphene has a long λ (~1 µm) at room temperature" and "carrier mobility of graphene was measured up to ~200,000 cm2 V−1 s−1 at room temperature" with BN encapsulation and 1D contacts; measured λ ≈ 0.7 µm at μ_e = 130,000 — [Nguyen 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41699-021-00269-2)
- "Typical MFPs in exfoliated graphene samples range between 40 and 70 nm" (on SiO2); hBN-encapsulated GD experiments: "mobilities ranging between 1×10^5 and 1.8×10^5 cm2V−1s−1 and corresponding to mean free paths between 700 and 900 nm"; Monte Carlo with intrinsic phonons only gives μ ≈ 10^5 "typical for hBN-encapsulated graphene", adding SiO2 remote phonons gives ≈10^4 "typical for exfoliated graphene on a SiO2 substrate", adding random potential scattering gives ≈10^3 (CVD on SiO2) — [Truccolo 2026 PDF](https://iris.unimore.it//retrieve/handle/11380/1403348/971969/TruccoloTED2026.pdf)
- "graphene offers unparalleled prospects as an MFP of about 10 µm can be achieved at room temperature [13], [14]" (refs: Bolotin 2008 suspended graphene; Banszerus 2016 CVD graphene in hBN, 28 µm ballistic at 1.8 K) — [Truccolo 2024 PDF](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)

- CVD/SiO2 GD: MFP 70 nm at V_GS = 0 (Wang 2021); exfoliated/SiO2: ≈45 nm (Zhu 2013, secondhand) — [PMC8399968](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399968/); [Truccolo 2024 PDF](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)

- Simulation-side treatment: Truccolo 2024 "deliberately neglect ... edge-roughness scattering" and assume "perfectly specular reflections at the graphene edges"; Stearns & Moddel 2021 "If a particle collides with a device boundary, it is reflected specularly" — [Truccolo 2024 PDF](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf); [Stearns & Moddel 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659/)

### Inferences
- For benchmark purposes, a realistic RT parameter set for hBN-encapsulated exfoliated graphene is μ ≈ 1–2×10^5 cm²/Vs, n ≈ 3×10^11–1.5×10^12 cm⁻², giving λ ≈ 0.7–1.5 µm; at cryogenic T (≲50 K) λ ≈ 3 µm but becomes width-limited for ≤1 µm channels. For CVD or exfoliated graphene on bare SiO2 use λ ≈ 45–100 nm.
- No GD experiment quantifies an edge specularity parameter; the consistent 2–4× shortfall of measured vs specular-wall-simulated asymmetry (Nguyen's comparison) is the best available empirical handle for calibrating a diffuse-fraction parameter in a simulator.

### Gaps
- No paper located gives a measured specularity coefficient or edge-roughness amplitude for etched hBN/graphene/hBN GD edges.
- Hall-bar MFP vs density curves in Auton 2016 (Fig. 1d–e) were not read numerically.
