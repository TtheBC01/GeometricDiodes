# Fabrication constraints for nanopatterned hBN/graphene/hBN devices with 20–100 nm features (necks, loops, branches) and their effect on ballistic transport

Scope note: this file covers fabrication realism only (what can be patterned, how the fabricated shape differs from CAD, how edges/contacts degrade ballistic transport, and which of those parameters a simulator must carry). Values marked **[computed]** are my own arithmetic from cited inputs; values marked **[recalled, unverified]** are from memory of the primary paper and could not be confirmed in fetched text; **[secondhand]** means the number came from a search snippet or summary rather than the primary text.

## KQ1. EBL + RIE of hBN/graphene/hBN: achieved minimum features, resists, etch chemistries, LER, corner rounding, CD uniformity

### Takeaway
State-of-the-art EBL+RIE on fully encapsulated graphene has demonstrated 23–26 nm necks (geometric diodes, 2024), ~40 nm lithographic antidots that come out 25–30 nm wide in the graphene plane, and ~200 nm constrictions with ~2 nm AFM edge roughness (cryo-etch). PMMA masks with CHF3/O2 or SF6 (Ar/SF6) plasmas are the norm; HSQ gives the best resolution/LER but is rarely used on encapsulated stacks. Nobody in the encapsulated-graphene literature reports a measured corner radius or chip-level CD distribution; the only quantitative "design vs fabricated" data are the Sandner antidots (40 nm → 25–30 nm) and the Auton rectifier (71 nm electrical vs 122–137 nm AFM).

### Cited Findings
- **Smallest necks in encapsulated graphene (geometric diodes):** h-BN/monolayer graphene/h-BN geometric diodes with neck widths of **23 nm and 26 nm** were made "by employing ... extremely precise electron beam lithography"; best device asymmetry ratio 1.97, zero-bias current responsivity 0.6 A/W, voltage responsivity 12,000 V/W (Wang et al., Adv. Electron. Mater. 10, 2300695 (2024), DOI 10.1002/aelm.202300695) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/4bd10bb4571448c382f1b1e72a72dbea); [Semantic Scholar record (open access, CC-BY, PDF behind 403 for automated fetch)](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1002/aelm.202300695?fields=title,abstract,year,venue,openAccessPdf,authors). The resist, etch chemistry, SEM-measured vs designed neck widths and device count are in the full text, which I could not fetch — **gap**.
- **Auton et al. ballistic rectifier (encapsulated graphene, Manchester, 2016):** contact lithography used a bilayer PMMA (495K 100 nm / 950K 100 nm); the **etch mask was a single 100 nm layer of 495K PMMA**, etch was **CHF3 + O2 in an Oxford RIE at RF 5 W / ICP 50 W**; edge contacts by etching through the whole BN–G–BN stack and depositing 3 nm Cr / 80 nm Au. Narrowest lead widths by AFM: **122 nm (source) and 137 nm (drain)**; width inferred electrically from Fermi-energy oscillations: **71 nm**. The paper says the device is not perfectly symmetric "due to the imperfection of nanolithography." — [Auton et al., Nat. Commun. 7, 11670 (2016), PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026)
- **Antidot lattices in encapsulated graphene (Sandner et al., 2015):** Hall bars by EBL + **CHF3/O2 RIE (40/6 sccm, 60 W)**; antidot lattice periods **50–250 nm**; **lithographic antidot diameter ≈ 40 nm, effective diameter in the graphene plane 25–30 nm** (from SEM and from commensurability-peak analysis, d/a ≤ 0.3), attributed to the **conical etch profile** through the top hBN. Encapsulation "excludes any contamination from process chemicals such as electron beam resist." — [Sandner et al., Nano Lett. 15, 8402 (2015), arXiv 1509.05207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207)
- **Terrés et al. constrictions (encapsulated, RWTH):** patterned by **RIE in SF6 with a ~10 nm Cr hard mask**, Cr-oxide residue stripped in TMAH (30–35 s); widths **W ≈ 230–850 nm** (eight devices 230–1000 nm); Cr/Au side contacts. — [Terrés et al., Nat. Commun. 7, 11528 (2016), arXiv 1603.00844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844)
- **Cryo-etched constrictions (Clericò et al., 2019):** encapsulated bar first defined by ICP SF6; constriction then cut by **Ar/SF6 (10/40 sccm) at −110 °C with a PMMA mask** ("less invasive" than a Cr mask); device **W ≃ 206 nm, L ≃ 200 nm**; **AFM edge roughness ≈ 2 nm** measured on a contour 15 nm below the top hBN surface (non-encapsulated HMDS-treated reference ≈ 1 nm). — [Clericò et al., Sci. Rep. 9, 13572 (2019), arXiv 1902.07459](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07459)
- **Process generalisation (RWTH review):** "dry transfer processes for making hBN-graphene-hBN sandwiches in combination with metal (Al) hard mask and SF6 or CHF3 based reactive ion etching turns out to allow for well controlled constrictions with high electronic quality." — [Somanchi et al., "From diffusive to ballistic transport in etched graphene constrictions and nanoribbons", arXiv 1706.09781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09781)
- **SF6 etch for quasi-1D edge contacts** to hBN-encapsulated (multilayer) graphene is also reported as a standard route. — [NIMS MDR record, MRS Advances 2025](https://mdr.nims.go.jp/datasets/a52c97ab-00f5-4f39-80ec-2c61d702fac2.md)
- **Edge roughness from SF6 RIE matters near CNP:** a 2023 RWTH dissertation on hBN-encapsulated single-layer graphene QPCs made by SF6 RIE states that "the rough edges due to physical etching play an important role in the quantized conductance characteristics of the QPC particularly around the charge neutrality point." **[secondhand, search snippet]** — [RWTH publications record 972605](https://publications.rwth-aachen.de/record/972605/files/972605.pdf)
- **HSQ resolution:** 7 nm lines at 20 nm pitch in 10 nm-thick HSQ (KOH-based developer) — [Grigorescu et al., SPIE 6519 (2007)](https://www.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/6519/65194A/Sub-10-nm-structures-written-in-ultra-thin-HSQ-resist/10.1117/12.725851.full); dense 4 nm lines at 25 nm pitch (hot development, 50 °C) and sub-10 nm lines with ~25:1 aspect ratio in 250 nm HSQ — [Glasgow, EIPBN 2011 5A-4](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2011/papers/5A-4.pdf); contrast up to 10 with salty developers in 115 nm HSQ — [Yang & Berggren, MIT 2007](https://qnn-rle.mit.edu/documents/Yang-2-2007.pdf); HSQ lines of 12 nm with 10 nm spaces directly on graphene for nanoribbons, with capillary collapse of dense arrays noted — [Cornell JVST 2012 GNR fabrication](https://djena.engineering.cornell.edu/papers/2012/jvst12_gnr_fabrication.pdf).
- **HSQ LER:** no peer-reviewed LER number for sub-10 nm HSQ lines surfaced; a vendor page claims "LER under 3 nm" without conditions **[unreliable]** — [vendor page](https://discheminc.com/?p=17887). A 2026 Cardiff IEEE TSM paper is LER-focused and notes HSQ's quality gain "comes at the cost of increased exposure times and reduced process flexibility" — [Cardiff ORCA 189280](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/189280/). HSQ surface roughness (which sets linewidth fluctuation) "drops rapidly with increased electron beam dose" **[secondhand]** — [EIPBN 2012 P04-09](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2012/papers/P04-09.pdf).
- **PMMA limits for narrow gaps:** PMMA's low contrast makes gaps below ~100 nm hard to produce reliably; a 65 nm gap over 100 µm required an extra lithography step **[III-V context, not graphene]** — [Glasgow EIPBN 2012 P04-12](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2012/papers/P04-12.pdf).
- **Corner rounding:** "conventional proximity correction constraints cause most features below 50 nm to look round and have no sharp corners" — [Argonne, EIPBN 2009 P-1F-14](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2009/papers/P-1F-14.pdf); corner-rounding correction methods exist for CDs ~35 nm (CEA patent) — [US 9607808](https://patents.justia.com/patent/9607808); Fraunhofer reports a "general limit of corner rounding correction" for shaped-beam tools — [Fraunhofer publica 385809](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/385809). No measured corner radius in nm for graphene devices was found.
- **CD uniformity:** sub-10 nm HSQ patterning "with the dimension controllability better than 5% of the critical dimension" **[single-process claim]** — [SPIE profile, Jedrasik](https://ebooks.spiedigitallibrary.org/profile/Piotr.Jedrasik-114987); an HSQ quantum-dot study reported that 80% of devices had dimensional variations < 5 nm **[secondhand, search snippet]** — [Warwick WRAP 179316](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/179316); shot-pitch quantisation alone can change a 60 nm square to 48 nm at 16 nm shot pitch — [Georgia Tech HSQ dose notes](https://nanolithography.gatech.edu/HSQ_dose.html).
- **Low-keV EBL** (2 keV, ultrathin HSQ) resolves 15 nm half-pitch with minimal long-range proximity effect — [Manfrinato et al., MIT 2011](https://qnn-rle.mit.edu/documents/manfrinato-1-2011-85.pdf).

### Inferences
- The realistic lower bound for a reproducible neck in an encapsulated stack with PMMA + fluorine RIE is ~20–25 nm lithographic (the 2024 diodes), and the in-plane graphene width will be smaller than the top-hBN opening because of the conical etch profile (Sandner: ~10–15 nm total shrinkage for ~40 nm openings; this depends on hBN thickness and etch anisotropy, so thin top hBN of 10–20 nm is favoured for small features).
- The CAD-to-fabricated transform for a simulator should include: (i) a uniform bias (shrink or grow) of several nm per edge, (ii) corner rounding with radius comparable to half the minimum feature (below ~50 nm CD, corners are effectively round per the Argonne statement), (iii) stochastic LER of ~1–3 nm RMS (cryo-etch ~2 nm; conventional RIE likely larger — Terrés' simulations needed 13 nm amplitude/5 nm correlation, see KQ2), and (iv) a device-to-device CD spread of order ±5 nm (10–20% of a 25 nm neck) **[inferred from the two uniformity snippets; weak evidence]**.
- HSQ would improve LER and gap resolution but sits on hBN, is removed with HF (which attacks hBN slowly) or left in place; the encapsulated-graphene community has stayed with PMMA or thin metal (Cr, Al) hard masks. A metal hard mask gives better etch selectivity but Terrés attributes microcracks/large edge roughness to that route.

### Gaps
- No fetched source gives the resist/etch recipe, designed-vs-SEM neck widths, or device count for the 23/26 nm geometric diodes (Wiley PDF returned 403). The KAUST thesis "Design and Process Development of Graphene-Based Geometric Diodes for Enhanced Performance" (H. Wang) likely contains these — [KAUST listing](https://ccsl.kaust.edu.sa/topics/nano-devices).
- No measured corner radius (nm) for any encapsulated-graphene device.
- No chip-level 3σ CD-uniformity figure for HSQ or PMMA at ~25 nm CD in any peer-reviewed source found.
- No peer-reviewed LER (3σ) number for HSQ or PMMA at the relevant CD.

## KQ2. Edge damage: width of the disordered/depleted region, effective electrical width vs lithographic width, transport-gap/CNP broadening below ~50 nm

### Takeaway
There is no consensus "dead edge width" for encapsulated etched graphene; the literature splits between (a) models in which the full lithographic width conducts but rough edges scatter (Terrés: c0 ≈ 0.56 explained by 13 nm roughness amplitude, 5 nm correlation length), and (b) evidence for an inactive edge region (Auton: electrical 71 nm vs AFM 122–137 nm, i.e. ~25–33 nm per side; Han 2007 on SiO2: W* ≈ 16 nm **[recalled]**; Sandner antidots 5–7.5 nm per side attributed to etch profile, not depletion). Below ~200 nm, the RWTH group states edge roughness becomes "dominant and unfortunately limiting"; below ~50 nm, localised edge states produce a transport gap/Coulomb-blockade region even in encapsulated or LAO-defined devices (20 nm BLG constriction → single quantum dot with >100 meV addition energy).

### Cited Findings
- **Terrés et al. (encapsulated, W = 230 nm):** "reflections at the rough edges of the constriction and not a reduction in active channel width is responsible" for the reduced conductance; zeroth-order Landauer fit gives **c0 ≈ 0.56** for the narrowest constriction; Fourier analysis of the conductance modulations recovers widths close to SEM. Simulations "include an edge roughness amplitude of ΔW = 0.2W" and "use a correlation length of 5 nm and an average disorder amplitude of 13 nm", the large roughness "probably due to microcracks at the edges". Deviation from G ∝ √n (the disordered/CNP region) begins at **n < 0.45×10^12 cm^-2 (electrons) and n < 0.75×10^12 cm^-2 (holes)** and widens after air exposure; kink positions shift between cool-downs. Renormalised v_F ≈ 1.5×10^6 m/s from a subband spacing 13.5 ± 2 meV. — [arXiv 1603.00844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844)
- **Auton et al. (encapsulated rectifier):** AFM lead widths 122/137 nm vs electrically inferred 71 nm; the authors suggest the ballistic region "does not extend all the way to the edge due to edge states and traps". — [PMC4895026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026)
- **Sandner et al. (encapsulated antidots):** lithographic d ≈ 40 nm, effective d = 25–30 nm, attributed to conical etch profile; paper states "a lack of a depletion region in graphene" and no edge doping; phase-coherence length 120–300 nm > period for a = 100 nm, read as "etched boundaries do not lead to severe phase-breaking." — [ar5iv 1509.05207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207)
- **Clericò et al. (cryo-etch, encapsulated, W ≈ 206 nm):** no separate electrical width given; transmission per mode t ≈ 0.9; simulations used the AFM-measured edge directly and matched the data; plateaus washed out by 25 K. — [arXiv 1902.07459](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07459)
- **Non-encapsulated low-roughness benchmark (Caridad et al. 2018):** Ar/O2 RIE (40 W, 40/5 sccm) through PMMA on HMDS-treated SiO2 gives "an edge roughness ≤ 1 nm" confirmed by TEM, for L = W ≈ 100 nm constrictions; µ ~ 20,000 cm²/Vs, l_mfp ≈ 200 nm at 4 K; "wider geometries have the added advantage of reducing the significance of edge disorder". — [Caridad et al., Nat. Commun. 9, 659 (2018), PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5811439/)
- **Same group, 85 nm constriction on SiO2:** c0 = 0.74 for W = 85 nm vs c0 ≈ 0.56 for encapsulated W = 230 nm (Terrés) — "we find a much lower roughness of the edge"; conductance is flat for W·k_F < 4 (experiment) and gapped for W·k_F < 2 (simulation of a perfect-edge constriction). — [arXiv 1802.09762](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.09762)
- **Width threshold for edge dominance (RWTH review):** "for (state-of-the-art) narrow (top-down) etched nanoribbons and constrictions (roughly < 200 nm) the influences of the rough edges are playing a dominant and unfortunately limiting role ... localized states and statistical Coulomb blockade are opening a mobility gap making the mean free path ... nearly meaningless. For the state-of-the-art etching technology, this puts a lower limit to nanoribbon and constriction width for observing quantum ballistic transport." Also: nanoribbons on hBN "of similar width etched from the same graphene flake showed strong variations in the quality and evolution of conductance", a "strong indication that edge disorder plays an important role"; a short HF dip (20 s) reduces edge disorder and shrinks the transport gap. — [arXiv 1706.09781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09781)
- **Charge localises along imperfect edges** in ultrashort constrictions; "the ribbons are too wide and the edges too disordered" for the ideal band gap to matter. — [Bischoff et al., arXiv 1406.5355](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.5355.pdf)
- **Transport gap scaling (SiO2 ribbons):** gap scales inversely with width, ~200 meV at ~15 nm width (4.2 K) **[secondhand via review]** — [Han et al., PRL 98, 206805 (2007), arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0702511); [review arXiv 1110.6557](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.6557). The fit E_g = α/(W − W*) with **W* ≈ 16 nm, α ≈ 0.2 eV·nm** (inactive edge width) is **[recalled, unverified in fetched text]**.
- Theory: "even for weak edge roughness, conductance steps are suppressed and transport gaps are induced" — [Mucciolo et al., arXiv 0806.3777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.3777); etched-ribbon gaps larger than α/W explained by localised states from edge roughness — [Molitor et al., arXiv 0811.0694](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0811.0694).
- **20 nm constriction in encapsulated bilayer graphene (AFM-LAO, no RIE):** wider constrictions bulk-like; narrowing "opens a transport gap"; narrowest (20 nm) forms a **single quantum dot with addition energies > 100 meV**, attributed to "edge disorder combined with quantum confinement". — [Rienstra et al., arXiv 2412.08758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08758)
- **Raman:** in etched GNRs 30–130 nm wide the D-line intensity depends only on the edge region (no bulk damage from RIE) — [Ryu et al., arXiv 1105.1001](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.1001); RIE-defined edges show a D peak, read as structural edge defects — [Hydrogen-plasma edge characterisation, arXiv 1903.07002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.07002).
- **He-ion patterning lateral damage:** in hBN-encapsulated graphene, lateral defect spread grows with dose, "reaching several tens of nanometers at the higher doses"; insulating regions need > 1×10^18 He+/cm² **[conference abstract]** — [EIPBN 2016 P044](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2016/papers/P044.pdf).

### Inferences
- For a simulator, two edge parameters are needed, not one: (1) a **geometric roughness** term (RMS amplitude 1–3 nm for optimised etches, up to ~10+ nm for conventional hard-mask RIE; correlation length ~5 nm per Terrés' fit — a few lattice constants to tens of nm), and (2) an **electrically inactive edge strip** (0 nm in the Terrés/Sandner reading; ~5–8 nm per side if the Sandner shrinkage is partly electrical; ~15 nm per side from Han's W* on SiO2; ~25–33 nm per side from Auton's electrical-vs-AFM discrepancy, though the latter may conflate lead geometry with edge depletion). A 25 nm lithographic neck could therefore have an electrical width anywhere between ~25 nm and ~0 nm depending on which picture holds — this is the single largest uncertainty for a Tesla-valve neck.
- Below ~50 nm, expect a transport gap of order 50–200 meV **[from Han scaling]** and Coulomb-blockade/QD behaviour at low T and low density; the device becomes a tunnel barrier rather than a ballistic nozzle near CNP. Operating at high density (n ≥ 2–5×10^12 cm^-2, λ_F ≈ 16–25 nm) keeps several modes open and pushes the Fermi level far above the edge-state band.
- hBN encapsulation protects the bulk during etching (no resist contact, high mobility retained — Sandner) but does **not** by itself make etched edges smooth: encapsulated SF6-etched constrictions (c0 ≈ 0.56 at 230 nm) compare unfavourably to non-encapsulated Ar/O2 PMMA-etched ones (c0 = 0.74 at 85 nm, ≤1 nm roughness). Cryo-etching is the one encapsulated process with measured ~2 nm roughness.

### Gaps
- No direct TEM measurement of the amorphised/dangling-bond edge width after fluorine RIE of encapsulated graphene was found.
- No paper gives a single "effective width = lithographic − x nm" relation for encapsulated graphene; the Han W* = 16 nm value is for SiO2-supported HSQ/O2-plasma ribbons.
- No encapsulated-graphene paper quantifies CNP broadening versus width systematically below 100 nm.

## KQ3. Mean free path at realistic conditions, width-dependent reduction, and whether hBN helps edges

### Takeaway
Encapsulated graphene gives room-temperature mobilities of 1–2×10^5 cm²/Vs and mean free paths of ~1–2 µm (λ_F ≈ 15–35 nm) at n = 1–5×10^12 cm^-2; at 1.5–4 K the mean free path is limited by device size (15–28 µm). Nanopatterning cuts the apparent mean free path to roughly the feature scale (antidot a = 100 nm → 160 nm apparent, ≥400 nm intrinsic), and for channels narrower than ~200 nm edge scattering dominates. hBN encapsulation preserves bulk mobility through etching but does not itself improve edge quality.

### Cited Findings
- **Wang et al. 2013 (edge contacts):** room-temperature mobility "in excess of 140,000 cm²/Vs" (15×15 µm device); at |n| = 4.5×10^12 cm^-2, sheet resistivity < ~40 Ω/sq and µ ≈ 40,000 cm²/Vs, "comparable to the acoustic-phonon-limited mobility"; at low T negative four-terminal resistance indicates quasi-ballistic transport "over at least 15 µm"; ballistic diagonal crossing implies mean free path "as large as 21 µm", i.e. µ ≈ 1,000,000 cm²/Vs at n ≈ 3×10^12 cm^-2; mean free path becomes device-size-limited below ~40 K and scales with device size from 1 to 15 µm. — [Wang et al., Science 342, 614 (2013), PDF](https://bioee.ee.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Science-2013-Wang-614-7.pdf)
- **Banszerus et al.:** CVD graphene encapsulated in hBN, µ up to 350,000 cm²/Vs (Sci. Adv. 2015) — [PMC4646786](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4646786); "carrier mobilities of up to 3 million cm²/(Vs)", ballistic transport "more than 28 µm" at low T and "over more than 1 µm up to 200 K" (bend-resistance measurement) — [Banszerus et al., Nano Lett. 2016, arXiv 1511.08601](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08601)
- **Auton et al.:** RT mobility 197,600 (electrons) / 106,000 (holes) cm²/Vs; mean free path stated to exceed 1 µm at RT; all seven devices > 100,000 cm²/Vs. — [PMC4895026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026)
- **Terrés / Clericò:** reference Hall-bar mobility ~150,000 cm²/Vs with l_mfp > 1 µm (Terrés, at ΔVg ≈ 4.6 V) — [ar5iv 1603.00844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844); ~150,000 cm²/Vs RT and l_m > 1 µm (Clericò) — [arXiv 1902.07459](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07459)
- **RWTH review design rule:** for µ = 300,000 cm²/Vs devices "with a length of around 1.0 µm exhibit (quantum) ballistic transport while for µ = 150,000 cm²/Vs the required device size is around 0.5 µm"; ballistic over ~1 µm requires n > 9×10^10 cm^-2 at high mobility; l_mfp = (ħ/e)·µ·√(πn). — [arXiv 1706.09781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09781)
- **Width-/pattern-dependent reduction (antidots, encapsulated):** unpatterned µ > 100,000 (one sample 300,000) cm²/Vs; a = 200 nm → apparent µ 35,000 cm²/Vs, apparent l_mfp ≈ 620 nm, intrinsic ≈ 1400 nm; a = 100 nm → apparent Hall µ ≈ 8,000 cm²/Vs at n = 2.8×10^12 cm^-2, apparent l_mfp 160 nm, intrinsic ≥ 400 nm. — [ar5iv 1509.05207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207)
- **hBN and edges:** Sandner: nanopatterning adds edge disorder and "an hBN substrate alone does not fix this", but etching fully encapsulated graphene is "more gentle" and preserves high mobility; the paper does not claim hBN protects the etched edge. — [ar5iv 1509.05207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207). RWTH review: GNRs on hBN had higher mobility than on SiO2 "However, the transport behavior of nanoribbons remained the same ... dominated by localized states". — [arXiv 1706.09781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09781)
- **Computed mean free paths [computed]** from l = (ħ/e)µ√(πn): µ = 140,000 at n = 1×10^12 → **1.6 µm** (λ_F = 35 nm); µ = 40,000 at 4.5×10^12 → **1.0 µm** (λ_F = 17 nm); µ = 200,000 at 1×10^12 → 2.3 µm; µ = 10^6 at 3×10^12 → 20 µm (consistent with Wang's 21 µm).

### Inferences
- Room temperature: l_bulk ≈ 1–2 µm is robust across groups; it is nearly density-independent above ~10^12 cm^-2 because phonon-limited µ falls as n rises. At 77 K, phonon scattering is weak and l is set by residual disorder/device size — several µm to >10 µm is plausible but no 77 K-specific number was found **[gap]**. At 1.5–4 K, l = 15–28 µm (size-limited).
- In a Tesla channel of width W ≈ 50–150 nm, the Sandner data suggest the apparent l collapses to ~1.5–3× the characteristic feature scale (a = 100 nm → 160 nm). A Matthiessen-type combination 1/l_eff = 1/l_bulk + 1/l_edge with l_edge ≈ W/(1−p) (p = specularity) reproduces this if p is low (≈0.3–0.5) for RIE edges; for ~2 nm-roughness cryo-etched edges p would be higher. **No graphene paper fetched reports a specularity parameter — this is an inference from the classical thin-film model.**
- Practical consequence: the Knudsen-like ratio must be computed with l_eff, not l_bulk; for W ~ 50 nm RIE channels, l_eff may be only ~100–200 nm at any temperature, so cryogenic bulk mean free paths buy little once the channel is that narrow.

### Gaps
- No encapsulated-graphene paper giving mobility or mean free path specifically at 77 K was found.
- No direct measurement of width-dependent mobility for encapsulated ribbons below 200 nm (the RWTH review says the mean free path concept breaks down there).
- No reported edge specularity parameter for etched graphene.

## KQ4. Contact resistance and dilution of two-terminal nonlinearity by a ~25 nm neck

### Takeaway
1D edge contacts reach ~100 Ω·µm (best) and are typically 100–200 Ω·µm, but real two-terminal nanodevices show total contact resistances of 0.4–3.2 kΩ. A 25 nm neck carries only 1–4 modes at n = 1–10×10^12 cm^-2, so its Sharvin resistance is 1.5–6.5 kΩ — comparable to measured contact resistances — meaning contacts can dilute a two-terminal asymmetry of 2.0 to 1.4–1.9 unless contact widths are wide or four-terminal/gated schemes are used.

### Cited Findings
- **Edge-contact resistance:** "the contact resistance is remarkably low (as low as 100 ohm·µm in some devices)"; simulation predicts 118 Ω·µm at E − E_CNP = 0.16 eV (n = 2.2×10^12 cm^-2) for a Cr(110)–O–graphene interface; contact resistance scales inversely with contact width and is "largely independent of temperature"; it "diverged near the charge neutrality point", with an additional local peak at finite negative density whose position shifts with a weak O2 plasma exposure before metallisation. Contacts: Cr adhesion layer (Cr/Pd/Au stack) — [Wang et al. 2013, PDF](https://bioee.ee.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Science-2013-Wang-614-7.pdf); news summary "100 Ohms per micron" — [graphene-info](https://www.graphene-info.com/graphene-encapsulated-bn-opens-way-edge-only-contacts-enables-cleanest-graphene-ever-made). A typical value ~150 Ω·µm is **[recalled, unverified]**.
- Edge contacts "even with the use of optical lithography, can reach contact resistances lower than 200 Ωµm" (Kelvin probe study) — [Duisburg-Essen DuEPublico 43906](https://duepublico2.uni-due.de/receive/duepublico_mods_00043906;lang=en;lang=en).
- **Measured in nanodevices:** Auton rectifier: "The contact resistances were measured between 0.8 and 1.4 kΩ"; device input resistance R_SD ≈ 22 kΩ, output R_LU ≈ 15 kΩ — [PMC4895026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026). Caridad 100 nm constrictions: 410 Ω and 518 Ω (designed < 600 Ω) — [PMC5811439](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5811439/). 85 nm SiO2 constriction: R_c = 3.2 kΩ — [arXiv 1802.09762](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1802.09762).
- **Landauer count for a neck [computed]:** N = W·k_F/π, G = (4e²/h)·N. For W = 25 nm: n = 5×10^11 → N = 1.0, R = 6.5 kΩ; n = 1×10^12 → N = 1.4, R ≈ 4.6 kΩ (6.5 kΩ quantised); n = 2×10^12 → N = 2.0, R = 3.2 kΩ; n = 5×10^12 → N = 3.2, R ≈ 2.0 kΩ; n = 1×10^13 → N = 4.5, R ≈ 1.4 kΩ. For W = 15 nm (25 nm minus a 5 nm dead strip per side): N < 1 below n ≈ 1.5×10^12 (one mode or pinched off). For W = 50 nm at 5×10^12: N = 6.3, R ≈ 1.0 kΩ.
- **Dilution [computed]:** with intrinsic forward/reverse neck resistances R and 2R (asymmetry 2.0) in series with total contact resistance R_c, measured asymmetry = (2R + R_c)/(R + R_c): R = 6.45 kΩ, R_c = 0.3 kΩ → 1.96; R_c = 1.0 kΩ → 1.87; R_c = 2.8 kΩ → 1.70. R = 2.0 kΩ: R_c = 0.3 → 1.87; 1.0 → 1.67; 2.8 → 1.42. R = 1.0 kΩ (50 nm neck, high n): R_c = 1.0 kΩ → 1.50.

### Inferences
- At 100–150 Ω·µm, a 1 µm-wide edge contact contributes 100–150 Ω, i.e. 200–300 Ω total — negligible against a 2–6 kΩ neck (dilution ≤ 5–10%). But measured two-terminal contact resistances in published nanodevices are 0.4–3.2 kΩ (narrow contacts, Cr/Au rather than Cr/Pd/Au, residues), which halves the apparent asymmetry in the worst case. The simulator should take R_c as an explicit series element (density-dependent, diverging near CNP) and report both intrinsic and two-terminal figures.
- Because the neck's resistance is quantised in units of h/4e² ≈ 6.45 kΩ and N changes by one mode for every Δn ≈ 10^12 cm^-2 at W = 25 nm, the I–V of a single-neck device at low T will show conductance steps and the geometric-diode "asymmetry" will be strongly density-dependent; a multi-stage Tesla device in series multiplies the neck resistance by the number of necks, which improves the neck:contact ratio but reduces current.
- Access/lead resistance of the wide graphene regions (ρ ≈ 40 Ω/sq at 4.5×10^12 cm^-2 RT) adds ~40 Ω per square of lead; for leads of a few squares this is <200 Ω — minor.

### Gaps
- No source reporting contact resistance specifically for the 23/26 nm geometric diodes (full text inaccessible).
- Density dependence of edge-contact resistance is shown only graphically in Wang 2013 (Fig. 1D); the exact values at n ≤ 10^12 cm^-2 are not in the fetched text.

## KQ5. How geometric-diode experiments report yield, variation, and the CAD-vs-fabricated gap

### Takeaway
Published geometric-diode papers barely quantify this: Auton (encapsulated) reports 7/7 devices with µ > 10^5 cm²/Vs and responsivity ~10,000 mV/mW for the group vs 23,000 for the best, admits asymmetry "due to the imperfection of nanolithography", and shows the electrical width (71 nm) is far below the AFM width (122–137 nm). CVD diodes give designed widths only, no measured widths, no device counts. The 2024 23/26 nm paper's full text (the most relevant) was not accessible.

### Cited Findings
- Auton et al.: "In total, seven devices have been made and they all showed a mobility higher than 100,000"; responsivity ~10,000 mV/mW typical vs 23,000 mV/mW optimum; no SEM-vs-design comparison, only Fermi-energy-derived width (71 nm) vs AFM (122/137 nm); device asymmetry attributed to "the imperfection of nanolithography"; ballistic region "does not extend all the way to the edge due to edge states and traps". — [PMC4895026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026)
- CVD monolayer geometric diodes (2021): designed necks 50/100/150/200/250 nm; AFM images shown but "No measured neck widths are given"; asymmetry 1.40 (50 nm), 1.25 (100 nm), ~1 (≥150 nm) at V_DS = 2 V; l_mfp = 70 nm at V_GS = 0; O2 plasma etch through PMMA 950K A4; not reported: device count, variation, yield, edge roughness, design-vs-fabricated comparison. — [Nanomaterials 2021, PMC8399968](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399968/)
- 2024 encapsulated diodes: two designs (23 and 26 nm necks), the better one 1.97 asymmetry; hBN encapsulation used explicitly because "the width of the neck must be on the order of l_c" **[secondhand]**; an earlier hBN-encapsulated diode reached asymmetry 1.66 with a 175 nm neck **[secondhand, search snippet, source not identified]** — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/4bd10bb4571448c382f1b1e72a72dbea)
- Zhu/Moddel 2013 (CVD graphene, J. Phys. D 46, 185101): fabrication details and neck width (≈75 nm **[recalled, unverified]**) not retrievable from fetched pages — [Colorado VIVO record](https://vivo.colorado.edu/display/pubid_27823)
- Non-diode statement on reproducibility: ribbons "of similar width etched from the same graphene flake showed strong variations in the quality and evolution of conductance" — [arXiv 1706.09781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09781); Terrés: kink positions shift between cool-downs and trap density changes after air exposure — [ar5iv 1603.00844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844)

### Inferences
- The only hard CAD-vs-device numbers in the encapsulated literature are: Sandner (40 nm drawn → 25–30 nm in graphene; −25 to −37%) and Auton (lead 122–137 nm physical → 71 nm electrical; −45 to −48%). A simulator should therefore sweep an "edge bias" of 5–30 nm per side and treat the asymmetry-vs-neck-width curve as the design output, not a single CAD geometry.
- Yield statements are absent; given that 23/26 nm necks are at the edge of PMMA/RIE capability, expect a sizeable fraction of pinched-off or shorted necks — **unquantified**.

### Gaps
- No geometric-diode paper gives neck-width statistics (mean/σ across devices), SEM-vs-CAD overlays, or yield.
- No statement explicitly attributing diode asymmetry loss to edge roughness was found (Auton's "edge states and traps" is the closest).

## KQ6. Alternative patterning routes with better edges or feature control

### Takeaway
Three routes could beat RIE edges: cryo-etching (≈2 nm roughness, already demonstrated on encapsulated stacks, scalable, EBL-defined so arbitrary Tesla geometries are possible); AFM local anodic oxidation (electrode-free, 15–20 nm features in encapsulated stacks, serial and slow, but can write arbitrary curves); and gate-defined confinement in bilayer graphene (no physical edges at all, 10 GΩ leakage, channel ≈ λ_F, but soft walls and bilayer physics). Helium-ion milling and anisotropic hydrogen-plasma etching are less promising for encapsulated multi-branch geometries (tens-of-nm lateral damage; hexagon-constrained crystallographic shapes).

### Cited Findings
- **Cryo-etching:** Ar/SF6 at −110 °C with PMMA mask on encapsulated graphene, ~2 nm AFM roughness, six 2e²/h plateaus at 3.1 K with t ≈ 0.9; authors argue scalability "could support more complex truly ballistic graphene devices." — [Clericò et al., arXiv 1902.07459](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07459)
- **Electrode-free AFM anodic oxidation:** Li et al., Nano Lett. 18, 8011 (2018), DOI 10.1021/acs.nanolett.8b04166 — [x-mol record](https://x-mol.com/paper/913457); extended to thick multicomponent vdW stacks by low-frequency tip–stage voltage, mechanism "remains to be uncovered" — [arXiv 2406.16354](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.16354); constrictions as narrow as 20 nm in encapsulated bilayer graphene (but the 20 nm one is a quantum dot) — [arXiv 2412.08758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08758); nanotrenches as narrow as 15 nm, GNR arrays at 50 nm period — [Nanoscale 2025, D5NR03204K](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/Content/ArticleLanding/2025/NR/D5NR03204K); LAO of graphite gates for nanoscale electrostatic control in ultraclean stacks — [arXiv 2204.10296](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.10296); review of LAO materials — [PMC12908326](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12908326/)
- **Gate-defined QPCs in bilayer graphene:** split gates with graphite back gate give depletion-gate leakage resistances ~10 GΩ, channel "about the size of the Fermi wavelength", quantised steps of 2e²/h and 4e²/h. — [Overweg et al., Nano Lett. 18, 553 (2018), arXiv 1707.09282](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.09282)
- **Helium-ion milling:** 10 nm gaps in suspended graphene; SiO2-supported graphene etched at lower dose but retained residual conductivity (hydrocarbon contamination) — [Lemme et al., ACS Nano 2009, arXiv 0905.4409](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.4409); in hBN-encapsulated graphene, 30 keV He+ at 7000 ions/nm² makes a 10 nm insulating barrier and gaps grow as ribbons narrow **[abstract]** — [Nanda et al., HZDR 24522](https://hzdr.de/publications/Publ-24522); lateral defect spread "several tens of nanometers" at high dose, encapsulation slows defect migration **[abstract]** — [EIPBN 2016 P044](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2016/papers/P044.pdf); encapsulated graphene tolerates 10^16 ions/cm² with sp² largely intact **[abstract]** — [EIPBN 2015 P03-06](https://eipbn.org/abstracts/2015/papers/P03-06.pdf); in MoS2, HIM/XeF2 damage extends ~150 nm past the edge and a thin hBN cap does not shrink it — [ORNL 2025](https://www.ornl.gov/publication/focused-helium-ion-beam-direct-patterning-monolayer-mos2-nanoribbon-field-effect)
- **Anisotropic hydrogen-plasma etching:** isotropic on SiO2 but highly anisotropic on hBN in the remote-plasma regime — [Hug et al., arXiv 1703.04762](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.04762); hBN-supported edges are ~40% zigzag / ~60% armchair-30° segments, "further optimization ... is required" — [arXiv 1903.07002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.07002); zigzag GNRs < 30 nm wide on hBN with high mobility and width-dependent gaps at 4 K — [npj 2D Mater. Appl. 2017](https://doaj.org/article/6716f4d3bc44416793f3e688bdacf5f5)
- **Edge-disorder reduction post-etch:** a 20 s dilute HF treatment suppresses the transport gap and shifts CNP toward zero (effective gap unchanged) — [arXiv 1706.09781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09781)

### Inferences
- For a Tesla valve (curved loops, re-entrant junctions, 20–100 nm features) the realistic candidates are **EBL + cryo-RIE** (arbitrary geometry, ~2 nm edges, parallel) and **AFM-LAO** (arbitrary geometry, ~15–20 nm minimum, serial, slow, and the 20 nm limit coincides with QD formation). Gate-defined confinement would avoid edges entirely but requires bilayer graphene, soft potential walls (gap-dependent, tens of nm smoothing), and cannot make a sharp re-entrant corner — the Tesla asymmetry would need to be re-derived for soft walls.
- Anisotropic etching yields crystallographic polygons, not loops; helium ions leave tens-of-nm damaged margins — both unsuitable for 25 nm necks in multi-branch layouts.

### Gaps
- No LAO or cryo-etch paper reports LER/correlation length in a form directly usable as a model parameter (only "~2 nm" roughness, no PSD).
- No demonstration of any curved/loop-type encapsulated graphene channel at sub-100 nm width with ballistic signatures was found.

## KQ7. Tesla-valve-specific geometry: minimum radii/widths, total length for 3–5 stages, achievable λ/L

### Takeaway
Taking 25 nm necks (the demonstrated minimum), ~75–150 nm main channels (so that the neck is a true constriction and loops are not edge-dominated) and loop radii ≥ channel width, each stage is ~300–600 nm long, so 3–5 stages span ~1–3 µm. Against bulk mean free paths of 1–2 µm (RT), ~several µm (77 K, unverified) and 15–28 µm (≤4 K), the device-level λ/L is ~0.3–1 at RT, ~2–5 at 77 K and ~7–20 at 4 K — but once edge scattering in 50–150 nm channels is included (apparent λ collapsing to ~1.5–3× feature size per Sandner), the room-temperature device is quasi-ballistic at best and only cryogenic operation keeps λ_eff > L_stage.

### Cited Findings
- Demonstrated minimum neck in encapsulated graphene: 23 nm — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/4bd10bb4571448c382f1b1e72a72dbea); minimum period with ballistic (commensurability) signatures: 50 nm; effective antidot diameter 25–30 nm from 40 nm drawn — [ar5iv 1509.05207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207); edge-dominated regime below ~200 nm width — [arXiv 1706.09781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.09781); quantum-dot formation at 20 nm (BLG, LAO) — [arXiv 2412.08758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08758).
- Bulk mean free paths: ~1–2 µm RT (Wang, Auton, Terrés, Clericò — see KQ3), 15–21 µm (Wang 2013, ≤40 K) and > 28 µm (Banszerus) at low T; ballistic over > 1 µm up to 200 K — [arXiv 1511.08601](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.08601).
- Apparent mean free path in a 100 nm-period antidot lattice: 160 nm (intrinsic ≥ 400 nm) — [ar5iv 1509.05207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207).
- Fermi wavelength λ_F = 2π/√(πn): 35 nm at 1×10^12, 25 nm at 2×10^12, 16 nm at 5×10^12, 11 nm at 1×10^13 cm^-2 **[computed]**.
- **Length budget [computed, assumption: stage length ≈ 4 × main-channel width, loop radius ≥ main width]:** W_neck = 25 nm, W_main = 75 nm, 3 stages → L ≈ 0.9 µm; 5 stages → 1.5 µm. W_neck = 50 nm, W_main = 150 nm, 3 stages → 1.8 µm; 5 stages → 3.0 µm. With λ_bulk = 1 µm (RT): λ/L = 1.1, 0.7, 0.6, 0.3 respectively; λ_bulk = 5 µm (77 K, assumed): 5.6, 3.3, 2.8, 1.7; λ_bulk = 20 µm (4 K): 22, 13, 11, 7.

### Inferences
- A neck must satisfy W_neck ≳ λ_F to carry ≥1 mode after subtracting any dead edge; at 25 nm lithographic width this forces n ≳ 2×10^12 cm^-2 (λ_F ≈ 25 nm) and makes N ≈ 2–4 modes — the device operates in the quantum (not semiclassical) regime, so a classical billiard/Monte-Carlo picture of the Tesla valve is not valid at the necks; a wave (tight-binding/NEGF or continuum Dirac) treatment with explicit edge disorder is required.
- Corner rounding ≳ 10 nm radius (half the minimum CD) will blunt every re-entrant Tesla junction; since the valve's rectification relies on sharp flow-splitting features, the simulator should treat corner radius as a swept parameter in the 5–25 nm range.
- Loops of 75–150 nm width and ≥75 nm radius are in the regime where Sandner's antidot data show apparent mean free paths of only 160–620 nm; so the per-stage transport is quasi-ballistic at RT and ballistic only at cryogenic T — and even there, edge roughness (not phonons) sets λ_eff.

### Gaps
- No published multi-loop or Tesla-like graphene channel exists to calibrate stage length or loop radius; the length budget above is a geometric assumption.
- No 77 K mean free path for encapsulated graphene was located; the 5 µm figure is an interpolation.

## KQ8. Which fabrication parameters must the simulator represent (synthesis)

### Takeaway
A credible simulator must carry at least eight non-ideal parameters, each with a sourced range: edge-roughness RMS amplitude (1–3 nm optimised; ~13 nm for conventional hard-mask RIE fits), roughness correlation length (~5 nm fitted; unmeasured PSD), an electrically inactive edge strip (0–30 nm per side, bimodal literature), per-edge CD bias from etch profile (−5 to −8 nm per side for ~40 nm openings), corner radius (≈ 5–25 nm, unmeasured), device-to-device CD spread (~±5 nm, weakly sourced), series contact resistance (0.2–3 kΩ total, density-dependent, diverging at CNP), and bulk mean free path/temperature (1–2 µm RT; 15–28 µm ≤4 K) plus an edge specularity or Matthiessen edge term.

### Cited Findings
- Edge roughness amplitude and correlation used to fit encapsulated constriction data: ΔW = 0.2W, average amplitude 13 nm, correlation length 5 nm — [ar5iv 1603.00844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844); measured ~2 nm (cryo-etch, encapsulated) — [arXiv 1902.07459](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07459); ≤1 nm by TEM (Ar/O2 RIE, SiO2) — [PMC5811439](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5811439/).
- Etch-profile CD bias: 40 nm → 25–30 nm — [ar5iv 1509.05207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.05207).
- Electrical vs physical width: 71 nm vs 122–137 nm — [PMC4895026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026); "not a reduction in active channel width" — [ar5iv 1603.00844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844).
- Contact resistance: 100–118 Ω·µm (edge contacts) — [Wang 2013 PDF](https://bioee.ee.columbia.edu/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/Science-2013-Wang-614-7.pdf); 0.8–1.4 kΩ (Auton), 0.41–0.52 kΩ (Caridad), 3.2 kΩ (85 nm SiO2 constriction) — see KQ4.
- Trap/edge-state density manifests as a density window of suppressed conductance (|n| < 0.45–0.75×10^12 cm^-2 at W = 230 nm) that widens with air exposure and shifts between cool-downs — [ar5iv 1603.00844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844).
- Temperature smearing of quantised features: plateaus visible < 10 K, gone by 25 K (Clericò); above ~10 K "only kinks ... survive" (Terrés). — [arXiv 1902.07459](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.07459); [ar5iv 1603.00844](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1603.00844)

### Inferences
- **Parameter table for the model (ranges from the sources above):**
  - Edge roughness RMS: 1–3 nm (best processes) / 5–15 nm (conventional RIE with hard mask); correlation length: 5 nm (fit) — sweep 2–20 nm.
  - Dead edge strip per side: 0 nm (Terrés/Sandner reading) to 5–8 nm (etch-profile shrink) to 15 nm (Han W*, SiO2) to 25–33 nm (Auton electrical) — sweep 0–20 nm; at 25 nm necks this spans "open" to "pinched off".
  - Corner radius: 5–25 nm (no measurement; below ~50 nm CD features print round).
  - CD spread: ±5 nm (weak evidence) → Monte-Carlo over neck widths 20–30 nm.
  - Contact resistance: 0.2–3 kΩ total series, density dependent; neck Sharvin resistance 1.4–6.5 kΩ for 25 nm at 1–10×10^12 cm^-2.
  - Bulk λ: 1–2 µm (RT), ~5 µm (77 K, assumed), 15–28 µm (≤4 K); edge-limited λ_eff ≈ (1.5–3)×W for RIE edges (antidot inference).
  - Operating density: ≥ 2×10^12 cm^-2 to keep ≥2 modes in a 25 nm neck and stay outside the edge-state window; CNP broadening of ~0.5–1×10^12 cm^-2 for ~200 nm channels, larger for narrower.
  - Temperature: quantised-mode features only below ~10–25 K; RT operation is semiclassical-with-edge-scattering in wide sections but still mode-limited at the neck.
- The fabricated geometry differs from CAD by: a uniform negative bias (5–8 nm per edge for 40 nm features), rounded corners (≥ several nm), stochastic LER (1–15 nm RMS depending on process), and an uncertain electrically inactive margin; for a 25 nm CAD neck the plausible electrical width range is ~0–25 nm, which is why the model must be run as a distribution rather than a single geometry.

### Gaps
- No source provides a measured edge power spectral density or specularity for etched graphene; the correlation length is a fitted, not measured, quantity.
- No source quantifies corner rounding or chip-level CD statistics for encapsulated graphene features at ~25 nm.
- The 2024 23/26 nm diode paper (most relevant single source) could not be read beyond its abstract.
