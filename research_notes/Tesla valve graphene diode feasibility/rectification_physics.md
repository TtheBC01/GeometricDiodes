# Fundamental physics constraints on rectification in passive two-terminal ballistic mesoscopic conductors (Tesla-valve-inspired geometries)

Notation used throughout: S = source, D = drain, T_DS(E) = total transmission probability from S into D at energy E, f_α(E) = Fermi function of reservoir α, V = V_S − V_D. Expansion of the two-terminal current: I = G_1 V + G_2 V^2 + G_3 V^3 + ... (Christen–Büttiker write G_11, G_111). Rectification = any even-in-V term, i.e. G_2 ≠ 0.

Sources were verified by reading the full text (arXiv/PDF) unless marked "abstract/snippet only".

## KQ1. What reciprocity + Landauer–Büttiker imply for a two-terminal device in linear response; why T_SD(E) = T_DS(E) for any elastic two-terminal scatterer; what this does and does not forbid

### Takeaway
For any phase-coherent elastic two-terminal scatterer, unitarity of the scattering matrix (current conservation) alone forces T_SD(E) = T_DS(E) at every energy and for any magnetic field; consequently the linear-response conductance is independent of current direction and even in B, and no two-terminal geometry, however asymmetric, can rectify in linear response. What is not forbidden is an even-in-V term at second and higher order, G_2 V^2, but that term is only consistent (gauge invariant) when the bias dependence of the internal electrostatic potential is included.

### Cited Findings
- Two-terminal scattering formula with energy- and bias-dependent transmission: I_α = (2e/h) Σ_β ∫ dE f(E − E_F − eV_β) A_αβ(E,{V_γ}), with A_αβ = Tr[1_α δ_αβ − s†_αβ s_αβ]; the linear conductance is G_αβ = (2e²/h)∫dE(−∂_E f) A_αβ with A evaluated at V = 0, and "The sum rules also follow from the unitarity of the scattering matrix, i.e. Σ_α A_αβ = Σ_β A_αβ = 0" — [Christen & Büttiker, Europhys. Lett. 35, 523 (1996), arXiv:cond-mat/9604029](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)
- Song states the identity Σ_{b≠a} T_{b←a}(E,B,{μ_γ}) = Σ_{b≠a} T_{a←b}(E,B,{μ_γ}) "holds for an arbitrary magnetic field B", which converts the current formula into I_a = (2e/h) Σ_{b≠a} ∫ [f(E−μ_a) − f(E−μ_b)] T_{b←a}(E,B,{μ_γ}) dE — [Song, Phys. Rev. B 59, 9806 (1999)](https://www.nano.physik.uni-muenchen.de/kotthaus/_assets/pdf/1999/99-09_Song_PRB.pdf)
- Onsager / reciprocity statement for mesoscopic conductors: "Microscopic reversibility thus requires that in the presence of a magnetic field B the conductance obeys G_αβ(B) = G_βα(−B) between contacts α and β. In particular, for a two-probe conductor the conductance is an even function of magnetic field G(B) = G(−B)." and "For linear transport, the scattering matrix is evaluated at the equilibrium potential U_eq(r) which is an even function of B. As a consequence, s_αβ(B) = s_βα(−B) at equilibrium." — [Sánchez & Büttiker, PRL 93, 106802 (2004), arXiv:cond-mat/0404387](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0404387)
- "For two-terminal conductors, the reciprocity theorem reduces to G12(B) = G12(−B), where G12 is the conductance with the current flowing from contact 1 to 2. The sign of the source-drain bias voltage and the orientation of the measurement leads are of no consequence in the linear response regime, such that G12(B) = G21(B). The reciprocity theorem breaks down in the non-linear response regime." — [Löfgren, Marlow, Shorubalko, Taylor, Omling, Samuelson, Linke, PRL 92, 046803 (2004), arXiv:cond-mat/0309106](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0309106)
- Multi-terminal reciprocity R_ij,kl(B) = R_kl,ij(−B) (citing Büttiker PRL 57, 1761 (1986)), applied to Song's four-terminal rectifier: "symmetry considerations and the Landauer-Büttiker formalism in its most common linear form do not allow for such a voltage drop" and "R_UL,SD = 0 and hence by means of Eq. (3) we would expect V_UL to vanish identically" — [Fleischmann & Geisel, PRL 89, 016804 (2002), arXiv:cond-mat/0112305](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0112305)
- Büttiker's 1988 paper "Symmetry of electrical conduction" (IBM J. Res. Dev. 32, 317) is described in a later comment as showing the two-terminal conductance is invariant under field reversal as a consequence of "unitarity of the scattering matrix, which itself results from the principle of charge conservation", valid also for incoherent two-terminal devices connected to phase-breaking reservoirs — [Bruno & Pareek comment, arXiv:cond-mat/0105506 (snippet only)](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0105506)
- Expansion to second order and the gauge-invariance sum rules: I_α = Σ_β G_αβ V_β + Σ_βγ G_αβγ V_β V_γ + …; G_αβγ = (e²/h)∫dE(−∂_E f)(2∂_{V_γ}A_αβ + e∂_E A_αβ δ_βγ); "Conservation of the total charge (gauge invariance) implies that the currents are conserved and that the currents be independent of a global voltage shift W: V_α → V_α + W … Σ_α G_αβγ = Σ_γ (G_αβγ + G_αγβ) = 0" and the gauge-invariance condition e∂_E A_αβ + Σ_γ ∂_{V_γ} A_αβ = 0 — [Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)
- For a two-terminal device, "Due to gauge invariance, the current I_1 = −I_2 = G_11 ΔV + G_111 (ΔV)² + ... depends only on the voltage difference ΔV = V_1 − V_2. The second-order nonlinearity is thus determined by the single rectification coefficient G_111." — [Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)

### Inferences
- Proof sketch of T_SD(E) = T_DS(E) that the note can state compactly: for N = 2 the two sum rules Σ_α A_αβ = 0 and Σ_β A_αβ = 0 give A_SS + A_DS = 0 and A_SS + A_SD = 0, hence A_SD = A_DS, i.e. Tr[s†_SD s_SD] = Tr[s†_DS s_DS] ≡ T(E). This uses only unitarity (flux conservation) of s(E), not time-reversal symmetry; it therefore holds in a magnetic field, for any shape, for specular or disordered elastic boundaries, and for any number of modes. Time-reversal symmetry additionally gives s(B) = s^T(−B), which is what underlies the multi-terminal Büttiker reciprocity; for two terminals it is redundant with unitarity.
- Consequence for Tesla-valve geometries: in linear response I(V) = G_1 V with G_1 = (2e²/h)∫dE(−∂_E f)T(E) regardless of which end is the "easy" direction. Pictures of asymmetric classical trajectories (electrons "funnelled" one way and "bounced back" the other) cannot change this, because the classical limit of unitarity is Liouville's theorem/detailed balance: at equal chemical potentials the phase-space flux S→D equals D→S mode-by-mode (this is the same statement as Knudsen-flow reciprocity, KQ5).
- What is NOT forbidden: (i) G_2 ≠ 0 at finite bias; (ii) transverse (four-terminal) rectified voltages V_LU ∝ I_SD² (ballistic rectifiers, KQ2); (iii) ac-driven ratchet currents (nonequilibrium, zero-mean drive). All are O(V²) or higher and vanish identically as V → 0.
- Important subtlety for numerical modellers: if one takes the equilibrium T(E) and simply integrates over a bias window with a symmetric split of the bias, I(V) = (2e/h)∫T(E)[f(E − μ − eV/2) − f(E − μ + eV/2)]dE, the result is exactly odd in V — energy dependence of T(E) alone does not rectify. Any even term obtained by choosing an asymmetric split (e.g. putting all of eV on one lead) is an artefact of a non-gauge-invariant assumption about where the potential drops. This is precisely the point of the Christen–Büttiker gauge-invariance condition (KQ2).

- Datta "Electronic Transport in Mesoscopic Systems" and Nazarov & Blanter were not accessible online; the unitarity argument above is reconstructed from the Christen–Büttiker sum rules and Song (1999), which cite Datta/Büttiker for the same result.

## KQ2. Correct account of nonlinear rectification in two-terminal ballistic/geometric diodes: T(E) + bias window, self-consistent potential (Christen–Büttiker 1996; Sánchez–Büttiker 2004), Song et al. 1998 ballistic rectifier, Fleischmann–Geisel 2002, Linke ratchets

### Takeaway
The leading rectification coefficient G_2 (= G_111) is set by how the bias redistributes the electrostatic potential inside the device (characteristic potentials u_γ(r)) multiplied by the sensitivity of transmission to that potential and to energy; it vanishes for a symmetric conductor and, in an unscreened limit, is proportional to the asymmetry of the local density of states times ∂_E T. The Song ballistic rectifier is a four-terminal device whose output V_LU ∝ I_SD² arises from a bias-induced collimation of the injected distribution (Song) and/or from the energy dependence of mode numbers in leads of unequal width (Fleischmann–Geisel); its linear-response output is zero by reciprocity.

### Cited Findings
- Christen–Büttiker's central formula: G_αβγ = (e³/h)∫dE(−∂_E f)∫d³x [δA_αβ/eδU(x)](2u_γ(x) − δ_βγ), where the characteristic potentials u_γ(x) = (∂U(x)/∂V_γ)_eq solve a Poisson equation with nonlocal screening, −Δu_α + 4πe²∫Π(x,y)u_α(y)d³y = 4πe² dn(x,α)/dE, driven by the injectivity dn(x,α)/dE (partial local DOS of carriers entering from contact α) — [Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)
- QPC rectification coefficient (two screening regions Ω_1, Ω_2): G_111 = (e²/h)( C_μ C(∂_{U1}T − ∂_{U2}T) + [C_μ(D_2⁻¹ − D_1⁻¹) − ½(T_1 − T_2)] e∂_E T ); "As one expects, G_111 vanishes for a symmetric barrier. Asymmetries can be due to differences of D_k, T_k, and ∂_{U_k}T for different k." Limits: screened (C ≪ D_k): G_111 = (e²/h)R(∂_{U1}T − ∂_{U2}T); unscreened (D_k ≪ C): G_111 = (e³/h)(D_2 − D_1)R∂_E T/(D_1 + D_2), "proportional to the asymmetry of the density of states" — [Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)
- Resonant-level example: "Without considering the self-consistent shift ΔU_0 one would get a wrong result which is not gauge invariant." Expansion gives G_111 = −(e³/h)(ΔΓ/Γ)∂_E T, i.e. the rectification is proportional to the barrier asymmetry ΔΓ = Γ_1 − Γ_2 times ∂_E T — [Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)
- Sánchez–Büttiker (same formalism, B-field asymmetry): "We emphasize that the asymmetry Φ ≠ 0 is generated in Eq. (2) only through the asymmetry in the electric potential: if the potential is even in B, Eq. (2) predicts for a two-terminal conductor a current that is even. Thus, a self-consistent description of charge redistribution is crucial." Also: "away from equilibrium (e.g., in nonlinear dc-transport) there are no fundamental reasons why the Onsager relations should hold" and asymmetries appear "only to the extent that the potential landscape in the interior of the conductor becomes an uneven function of magnetic field" — [Sánchez & Büttiker 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0404387)
- Löfgren et al.: "In the general case, where the conductor has no symmetry (e.g. due to disorder), G12(V) ≠ G12(−V). This is because, if an applied voltage modifies the asymmetric device potential, the resulting device potential depends on the voltage sign." For "rigid" devices G12(V,B) = G21(−V,B); for left–right symmetric rigid devices G12(V,B) = G12(−V,−B); "the absence of LR-symmetry implies that, in the non-linear regime G12(V) ≠ G12(−V), regardless of the magnetic field sign" — [Löfgren et al. 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0309106)
- Song ballistic rectifier (GaAs/AlGaAs, triangular antidot in a four-terminal cross; l_e ≈ 6 µm at 4.2 K, W_SD ≈ 0.4 µm, W_LU ≈ 2.9 µm): "Negative voltages were observed between the L and U probes V_LU independent of the direction of the source-drain current I_SD." Mechanism in Song's model: the self-consistent field "mainly exists at the geometric narrowing or spreading including the entrances and exits of the leads"; the lead current adds an excess velocity Δv along the channel so the ejection angle changes from arctan(v_y/v_x) to arctan[v_y/(v_x ± Δv)], a "collimation/decollimation effect"; T_{L←S} increases by ≈6.4 % and T_{U←S} decreases by ≈34 % from I_SD = 0 to −37 µA; result R_SD,LU = (3/2)(h/e²)(sin θ_e − sin θ_0)/(2N_LU − 3N_SD(1 − sin θ_0)²) with no adjustable parameters, ≈3× the measured V_LU before correcting for impurity scattering in the 5 µm L/U channels — [Song, PRB 59, 9806 (1999)](https://www.nano.physik.uni-muenchen.de/kotthaus/_assets/pdf/1999/99-09_Song_PRB.pdf)
- Small-current limit is quadratic: V_LU = −(3πħ²/2e³E_F)·N_SD(sin 2θ_0) I_SD² / (2N_LU − 3N_SD(1 − sin θ_0)²), valid "for sufficiently small currents at finite temperature, meaning that there is no threshold voltage for the ballistic rectifier"; room-temperature operation and 50 GHz response demonstrated; dc output ∝ ac power — [Song, Appl. Phys. A 75, 229 (2002)](https://www.nano.physik.uni-muenchen.de/kotthaus/_assets/pdf/2002/02_11_Song_Appl_Phys.A.pdf)
- Fleischmann–Geisel alternative: "the necessary nonlinearity arises in the transport equations, if we allow for varying numbers of modes in the leads", M(E) = Int[W√(2m*E)/(ħπ)]; a voltage probe fed through a narrow (M = const) lead floats at μ_P = (μ_S + μ_D)/2, but through a wide quasi-classical lead (M ∝ √E) at μ_P = [(μ_S^{3/2} + μ_D^{3/2})/2]^{2/3} > (μ_S + μ_D)/2; the difference between two such probes gives V_UL, "Reverting the current yields the same voltage drop due to symmetry and thus we achieve the rectification of the signal." They predict sign changes/oscillations of V_UL when a new mode opens — [Fleischmann & Geisel 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0112305)
- Fleischmann–Geisel describe the Song approach as "a phenomenological ansatz" assuming "dissipation within the sample leading to self-consistent electric fields and a current dependence of the transmission probabilities" — [Fleischmann & Geisel 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0112305)
- Büttiker & Sánchez published a Comment on Fleischmann–Geisel, PRL 90, 119701 (2003), with a Reply PRL 90, 119702 (2003) — [Geneva archive record (abstract/record only)](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:4195)
- Graphene four-terminal ballistic rectifier (Auton et al.): "asymmetric four-terminal cross-junction that relies on the 'billiard ball' motion of carriers"; "the rectifying functionality is based on the collimating effect of carriers induced by the applied current"; room-temperature mobility ≈200,000 cm²/Vs, mean free path >1 µm; responsivity 23,000 mV/mW, NEP 0.64 pW/Hz^½; "The dependence is quite linear at low input power, as expected from the extended Büttiker–Landauer formalism" (dc output linear in ac power = quadratic in voltage); low-T output oscillations attributed to lateral quantization k_⊥W_SD = πm — [Auton et al., Nat. Commun. 7, 11670 (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Ratchets: Linke et al., "Experimental Tunneling Ratchets", Science 286, 2314 (1999) — an asymmetric (triangular) quantum dot in a GaAs/AlGaAs 2DEG driven by a zero-mean square-wave source–drain voltage produces a net dc current whose direction reverses with temperature when tunnelling contributes — [Hänggi & Marchesoni review, arXiv:0807.1283 (snippet)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0807.1283); Song's review frames all these devices as electron ratchets: "a system with a broken spatial symmetry is taken out of equilibrium by … an external fluctuation (i.e. the time average is zero), a net flow of particles along a certain direction may occur" — [Song 2002](https://www.nano.physik.uni-muenchen.de/kotthaus/_assets/pdf/2002/02_11_Song_Appl_Phys.A.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Christen–Büttiker G_111 formula is the correct "settling equation" for a two-terminal Tesla-valve channel: G_2 ≠ 0 requires both (i) a spatially asymmetric response of the internal potential to bias (u_S(x) ≠ 1 − u_D(x) mirror-wise) and (ii) sensitivity of T to that potential or to energy. A purely geometric asymmetry of hard walls enters only through where the injected charge piles up (injectivity asymmetry D_1 ≠ D_2, e.g. near a neck) — it does not act on its own.
- Rocked ratchets (Linke) and the Song/Auton rectifiers are the experimental proof that asymmetric ballistic geometries do produce even-order response, but all of them are either four-terminal (transverse output) or ac-driven, and all report an output quadratic in drive at small signal (no threshold, no linear-response diode action). The user's Tesla valve, as a two-terminal dc element, is in the strictly harder class where only G_2 V² and higher are available.
- The Fleischmann–Geisel vs Büttiker–Sánchez dispute is directly relevant to a modeller: Fleischmann–Geisel obtain rectification without a self-consistent potential by letting mode numbers depend on energy while assigning the full chemical-potential differences to the leads; the Christen–Büttiker/Sánchez–Büttiker line insists that any such non-self-consistent assignment is not gauge invariant. A Tesla-valve model that puts the bias drop in by hand (e.g. uniform field, or all drop at the neck) will inherit the same objection.

### Gaps
- The text of the Büttiker–Sánchez Comment (PRL 90, 119701) and the Fleischmann–Geisel Reply (PRL 90, 119702) could not be accessed; the specific criticism is inferred from Christen–Büttiker/Sánchez–Büttiker's stated gauge-invariance requirement, not verified.
- Song et al. PRL 80, 3831 (1998) itself was not fetched; its content is taken from Song PRB 1999, Song 2002 and Fleischmann–Geisel's description of the experiment.

## KQ3. In the semiclassical Monte Carlo picture of the geometric-diode literature, what actually produces the asymmetry? Which of (a) shape acting on a drifted distribution, (b) diffuse edge scattering, (c) field-dependent Fermi surface, (d) bias-dependent electrostatics survives detailed balance?

### Takeaway
In the Moddel-group and derivative Monte Carlo models the asymmetry is generated by the applied electric field accelerating carriers between collisions inside an asymmetric hard-wall boundary (mechanism (a)/(c): the field drifts the injected distribution, and the funnel/shoulder geometry then transmits the drifted distribution differently for the two field signs); with zero field the asymmetry ratio is exactly 1 and it also tends to 1 as the mean free path → 0. Self-consistent electrostatics (d), when added, substantially counteracts the geometric effect. Diffuse (cosine-law) wall scattering (b) does not by itself create asymmetry, being detailed-balance preserving.

### Cited Findings
- Moddel group model statement: "The operation of geometric diodes can be visualized using the Drude model for charge conduction … Charges undergo ballistic transport until they suffer a collision or are reflected off an edge. In the simplest case we assume that the reflections are specular. After each collision the carriers are launched again, moving at the Fermi velocity in a random direction modified slightly by applied electric fields. Leftward-moving carriers in the right-hand region are likely to encounter a vertical edge that causes them to reverse direction. On the other hand, rightward moving carriers in the left-hand region are likely to encounter a diagonal edge that funnels them towards the aperture. In this way carriers move more easily from left to right. Forward bias corresponds to an applied field that drives carriers in their preferred direction"; "For the asymmetric geometry to be effective … the carrier mean-free path length (MFPL) must be on the order of or greater than the neck width"; a symmetric control device "found no electrical asymmetry"; measured graphene device: neck 75 nm, shoulder 400 nm; polarity reverses with carrier type under gating; "Although the nonlinearity of these devices is small, the responsivity … is already substantial" — [Zhu, Joshi, Moddel, Solid State Commun. 152, 1842 (2012)](https://www.colorado.edu/faculty/moddel/sites/default/files/attached-files/moddel12.pdf)
- Particle-in-cell Monte Carlo (Stearns & Moddel 2021): "Each macroparticle is initiated with a randomly directed velocity vector with magnitude equal to v_F"; "The input voltage is set as a boundary condition between the two electrodes"; Poisson solved at every mesh node, "self-consistent electric field computation at every time step"; "If a particle collides with a device boundary, it is reflected specularly and continues propagating"; collision times exponential with τ = λ_MFP/v_F; earlier method (Zhu et al.) used a fixed field "perturbing their initial randomly directed momentum"; asymmetry = |I(+V_0)|/|I(−V_0)|; as λ_MFP → 0 "the asymmetry approaches 1"; for a 100 nm neck the asymmetry peaks at λ_peak ≈ 600 nm and decreases for longer MFP because unscattered carriers reflected from the far boundary return through the neck in reverse bias; the Z-diode shows reduced asymmetry at ≤0.1 V ("turn-on"), reason "not yet clear" — [Stearns & Moddel, Nanomaterials 11, 2361 (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659/)
- Truccolo et al. (self-consistent MC/3-D Poisson, ballistic graphene, W_c = 100 nm, W_n = 10 nm): in the uniform-field regime "T_SD tends to W_n/W_C = 0.1 for negative values of V_DS … while T_SD and T_DS tend to zero" for the opposite sign; "T_DS exceeds W_n/W_C for V_DS > 0, getting larger and larger as V_DS increases, consistently with … the enhancement of the geometric funnel effect"; current asymmetry A = I_DS(+V)/I_DS(−V) ≃ T_DS(+V)/T_SD(−V); "In the limit of large V_DS, the current asymmetry coefficient should tend to W_d/W_n … the maximum achievable current asymmetry is simply given by the ratio between the base and the neck of the funnel." Abstract: "while the current asymmetry is mainly induced by ballistic transport in the asymmetric structure, the electrostatics plays a relevant role that tends to substantially counterbalance the geometrical effect." Experimental context quoted: A ≈ 1.3 at 1.5 V (exfoliated, MFP ≈ 45 nm), A ≈ 1.1 for CVD graphene — [Truccolo, Palestri, Esseni, Boscolo, Midrio, IEEE Trans. Electron Devices 71, 1294 (2024)](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Truccolo et al. also note bipolar transport near the Dirac point is "detrimental to the current asymmetry … electrons and holes are driven in opposite directions by a given electric field, so that for both the positive and negative fields we have carriers traveling across the neck region in opposite directions" — [Truccolo et al. 2024](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- CVD-graphene geometric diode MC: "Particles were reflected specularly off device boundaries"; MFP 70 nm; field perturbs velocities, "velocity reset in a random direction" at the collision rate; asymmetry 1.40 (50 nm neck) and 1.25 (100 nm neck) at V_DS = 2 V, ≈1 for wider necks — [Nanomaterials/PMC8399968](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399968/)
- Song's semiclassical explanation of the Song rectifier is explicitly a drifted (collimated) injected distribution: at I_SD = 0 the angular distribution of ejected ballistic electrons is P(θ) = ½cos θ, and the self-consistent field at the lead exits adds Δv so "the electrons ejected out of the S channel will be collimated"; the relevant transmissions become functions of the lead current, T_{b←a}(I_a) — [Song 1999](https://www.nano.physik.uni-muenchen.de/kotthaus/_assets/pdf/1999/99-09_Song_PRB.pdf)
- Zero-net-flux under equal reservoirs with diffuse or specular walls (gas analogue, used here for mechanism (b)): for an isothermal ratchet wall "The second law of thermodynamics dictates that in the case ΔT = 0, this sum [of axial momentum fluxes from the inclined and vertical faces] vanishes. Otherwise, the gas above the Knudsen layer would experience a net force … and it would be possible to extract work simply by cooling a single heat bath" — [Shahabi, Baier, Roohi, Hardt, Sci. Rep. 7, 41412 (2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5269730/)
- Cosine-law (diffuse) reflection emerges even from purely specular reflection in chaotic billiard geometries: "One can even obtain such 'ergodic' behavior with a pure specular law of reflection, for a non-trivial geometry of the surrounding walls. Indeed, in the limiting case of billiard problems … one generically obtains the cosine law of reflection whereas incident and reflected angles are specularly related" — [Celestini & Mortessagne, PRE 2008, arXiv:0708.1117](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.1117)

### Inferences
- Decomposition for the user's question. (a) Shape acting on a shifted distribution: this is the operative mechanism in every MC paper read — the field (uniform in Zhu/Moddel, self-consistent in Stearns–Moddel and Truccolo) tilts trajectories between scattering events, and the asymmetric boundary transmits the tilted distribution unequally; it is intrinsically O(E-field), i.e. nonlinear, and gives A → 1 as V → 0 (Stearns–Moddel's low-bias behaviour, Truccolo's T_SD = T_DS at V = 0 implied by A ≃ T_DS(+V)/T_SD(−V) → 1). (c) "Field-dependent Fermi surface" is the same physics in momentum-space language (the Fermi circle shifted by the drift; Song's Δv). (d) Electrostatics: in a gauge-invariant theory this is what makes (a) well-defined — the field that tilts trajectories is the self-consistent one; Truccolo's finding that it "substantially counterbalance[s]" the geometric effect means uniform-field MC overestimates asymmetry. (b) Diffuse edge scattering: a cosine-law wall is the canonical detailed-balance-preserving boundary (Knudsen); it randomises but does not bias trajectories, so it cannot by itself rectify; what it does is shorten the effective ballistic length and so suppress (a). In equilibrium (V = 0) none of (a)–(d) gives a current — all are driven terms.
- The frequently quoted MC statement that carriers "see" the asymmetry only when λ_MFP ≳ neck width should be read as a statement about the magnitude of G_2, not about a linear diode: it is the condition for the drifted distribution to reach the neck with its tilt intact.
- The Stearns–Moddel "optimum at finite MFP" and Truccolo's "A_max = W_d/W_n in the uniform-field limit" are two different regularisations of the same fact: in the strictly ballistic limit with no field inside the device, T_SD = T_DS exactly (KQ1), so the asymmetry must come from the field inside the device, whose effect saturates geometrically.

## KQ4. Published "electronic / quantum / phonon / magnon Tesla valves": mechanism and findings; fluid Tesla valves work via inertial nonlinearity, Stokes limit has diodicity 1

### Takeaway
Every verified solid-state Tesla valve that rectifies (graphene electrons, Geurs 2020; GaAs electrons, Sarypov 2026; graphite phonons, Huang/Nomura 2024) does so in the hydrodynamic (electron–electron or phonon normal-scattering dominated) regime and explicitly not in the ballistic or diffusive regimes; the rectification is nonlinear in drive (appears above a threshold current in GaAs, tracks Reynolds number), mirroring the fluid case where diodicity is 1 for Re ≲ 100 and turns on only with inertial/turbulent flow. No magnon Tesla valve was found; the one "quantum Tesla valve" claim is a 2017 conference abstract without accessible method.

### Cited Findings
- Fluid Tesla valve: diodicity Di = R_reverse/R_forward (hydraulic resistances); "valving action is absent at low Re ≪ 1 for which flows are reversible"; "For low Re, Di is close to unity and remains so up until Re ≈ 100"; "diodicity turns on abruptly at Reynolds number Re ≈ 200"; Di ≈ 2 for Re = 300–1500; turn-on "is accompanied by the onset of nonlinear scaling of pressure drop with flow rate" and "diodic function is closely linked to a transition to turbulent flow" — [Nguyen, Abouezzi, Ristroph, Nat. Commun. 12, 2884 (2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8128925)
- Graphene electronic Tesla valve (hBN-encapsulated, graphite back gate, single loop, four-terminal dc, D = R_f/R_b): "It exhibits nonreciprocal transport and can be regarded as an electronic viscous diode. Rectification occurs at carrier densities and temperatures consistent with the hydrodynamic regime, and disappears both in the ballistic and diffusive transport regimes." Viscous signal: "one area with D ≈ 1.05 stands out. It encompasses intermediate temperatures (20 K < T < 100 K) and large hole concentrations (n < −5·10¹¹ cm⁻²)"; the sign of the viscous contribution flips with carrier species. Stated regime condition: l_ee ≪ W ≪ l_imp, l_ph. Mechanism: "In one direction (top panel), the flow bifurcates and the two streams recombine further along the conduit at a large angle, causing a loss of energy … The increased velocity gradient causes flow resistance and dissipation in the viscous fluid". Ballistic transport: "Momentum is then conserved for individual electrons but no collective response can occur." — [Geurs, Kim, Watanabe, Taniguchi, Moon, Smet, arXiv:2008.04862 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04862)
- Geurs et al. also identify a trivial, regime-independent rectification: near the charge-neutrality point "the local carrier density becomes a function of the position as well as of the sign and magnitude of the applied current. This is reminiscent of the spatially varying carrier density in the channel of a field-effect transistor and causes unintended rectification irrespective of temperature and the transport regime. The size of the effect scales inversely with the applied gate voltage." — [Geurs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04862)
- GaAs electron Tesla valve (n = 6.8×10¹¹ cm⁻², μ ≈ 10⁶ cm²/Vs, channel widths 2.5 and 5 µm, multi-loop, T = 4–70 K, four-probe dc): "Below I ≈ 300 µA, the I-V curves coincide with those corresponding to forward bias, but, at higher currents, they sharply diverge"; "as reverse current exceeds the value of 250–350 µA, we observe sharp increase of the resistance while the forward resistance remains low (<1 kΩ) and almost unchanged indicating linear transport"; Di = R_reverse/R_forward "rising steeply to values as high as Di ≈ 40 in wider valve"; "Micron-scaled width of the samples supports hydrodynamic transport while excluding ballistic mechanisms responsible for rectification in so-called 'ballistic rectifiers' … Those devices rely on the collimation or reflection of narrow electron beams and therefore require submicron dimensions"; estimated Re ≳ 7 at I ≳ 350 µA; diodicity suppressed above ≈20 K where l_MR becomes comparable to W; they describe the only prior graphene study as reporting "a vanishing rectification of ∼5 %" — [Sarypov, Pokhabov, Pogosov, Zhdanov, Shevyrin, Bakarov, "Electron Tesla valve", arXiv:2603.16443 (v2, Apr 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16443)
- Graphite thermal (phonon) Tesla valve: "we experimentally observe a discernible 15.2 % difference in thermal conductivity between opposite directions at 45 K" (D = κ_f/κ_r ≈ 1.15); rectification in the 25–60 K range; silicon Tesla valves of the same geometry: "the diodicity values stay constant around 1 … Thus, hydrodynamic phonon behaviour is required to observe thermal rectification in a Tesla valve"; in the ballistic regime (≈10 K) forward/reverse conductivities 4.18 vs 4.14 W m⁻¹K⁻¹ because "ballistic phonons excited by the heat source in random directions can traverse the entire structure"; diffusive (Fourier) regime gives D ≈ 1; their phonon Monte Carlo (specular/diffuse boundaries via Soffer specularity, no normal scattering) "include[s] no hydrodynamic effects" — [Huang et al., Nature 634, 1086 (2024), PMC version](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11525177) ([Nature link](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08052-1))
- "Quantum Tesla valve" claim: "Investigation of transmission properties of a simple quantum Tesla valve", Chattrapiban, Mongkolkiattichai, Maichum, Siam Physics Congress, 25 May 2017: single atom through structures "built from a few static equiangular triangles" with claimed extinction ratio (forward/backward transmission) > 50; the abstract gives no method, no statement about reciprocity or the initial state — [indico record (abstract only)](https://indico.global/event/8304/contributions/76244/)
- "Thermal noise rectification with graphene" (2025, Elsevier journal) is reported by search snippet to describe a graphene ballistic diode "based on the Tesla valve structure" harvesting thermal noise to a few µV from a resistor input — [ScienceDirect S2211285525000461 (access blocked, snippet only)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211285525000461)
- Magnon/spin-wave Tesla valve: searches ("Tesla valve magnon", "Tesla valve spin wave") returned only unrelated magnon "spin valves" and a thermo-magnonic diode (arXiv:1311.0123); no Tesla-geometry magnon device was found.

### Inferences
- The three verified solid-state Tesla valves confirm the KQ1 constraint empirically: in the single-particle ballistic limit (graphite at 10 K; graphene at 2 K; GaAs below 300 µA) diodicity is 1 within error. Rectification requires a nonlinear collective term — electron viscosity/inertia (the (v·∇)v and viscous terms of the electron Navier–Stokes equation) — exactly as in fluids, where Stokes flow (linear, kinematically reversible) gives Di = 1.
- Magnitudes: viscous-window graphene ≈5 % (Di ≈ 1.05), GaAs with turbulence-like threshold Di up to ≈40 but only above ≈300 µA at ≤20 K. Neither is a small-signal diode: in the GaAs device the forward and reverse I-V are identical in the linear regime by construction of the data.
- The Geurs "trivial" background mechanism (bias-dependent local density under a fixed gate) is a concrete realisation of KQ3 mechanism (d) and is present in any gated two-terminal graphene device; it must be subtracted before attributing any observed asymmetry to geometry.
- The 2017 "quantum Tesla valve" extinction ratio > 50 cannot be a stationary two-terminal transmission ratio (KQ1 forbids T_fwd ≠ T_bwd); if real it must refer to a transient wavepacket calculation with non-mirror-image initial states, to a multi-exit geometry, or to a classical-vs-quantum comparison; treat as unverified.
- Caveat on the user's premise: a Tesla valve in a fluid is a nonlinear device in Re; the electron analogue is likewise nonlinear in current. A Tesla-valve geometry therefore cannot be expected to "rectify electrons" by shape alone in the ballistic regime — the reported electronic Tesla valves are hydrodynamic diodes, which is the opposite transport regime from ballistic.

### Gaps
- Full text of the 2025 "Thermal noise rectification with graphene" paper (authors, geometry, model) was inaccessible; it is the only item found that claims a ballistic graphene Tesla-valve-type diode and should be obtained and scrutinised against KQ1/KQ3 before being relied upon.
- The Sarypov et al. preprint is not peer reviewed (arXiv v2, April 2026); its turbulence interpretation is described by the authors as "plausible"/"points to", not demonstrated.
- No electron-Tesla-valve theory paper (Navier–Stokes electron simulation of a Tesla geometry) was found; Geurs et al. only show a classical fluid simulation.

## KQ5. The ballistic Knudsen-flow analogue: rarefied gas through an asymmetric channel with specular (or cosine-law) walls — is there rectification? When do Knudsen pumps/ratchets work?

### Takeaway
In free-molecular flow the steady-state (equal pressure, equal temperature) condition forces the forward and reverse transmission probabilities of any channel, whatever its shape, to be related by equilibrium (A_1 W_12 = A_2 W_21, so the conductance is the same both ways); no net flow and no diodicity arise from geometry alone with either specular or cosine-law walls. Knudsen pumps and ratchet channels pump only when a temperature difference is imposed, and the asymmetric (ratchet) geometry then sets the direction; at ΔT = 0 the second law forbids a net flow.

### Cited Findings
- NASA free-molecule flow analysis of converging/diverging tubes and slots: "A relation between the wall flux values for flow through any particular configuration in the forward or reverse directions may also be developed from consideration of equilibrium requirements … If the gas in the total enclosure is considered to be in a steady-state, the arrival rates n_1 and n_4, and indeed the arrival rates anywhere within the enclosure, must all be equal … This relation, which does not depend on the shape of the opening, or passageway, provides a convenient tool for evaluation of reverse flow through a particular configuration (e.g., tube or slot) once the flow in the forward direction has been calculated"; and "Under steady-state conditions in the enclosure … there must be no net flow through the tube, or slot, connecting the two chambers. [From this] transmission probabilities through the same configuration in the forward and reverse directions can be derived." — [Richley & Reynolds, NASA TN D-2330 (1964)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19640011357/downloads/19640011357.pdf)
- Ratchet channel (Shahabi et al.): with an isothermal, fully diffuse wall "there is no net momentum exchange in x-direction between the gas and the fully diffuse isothermal wall"; for ΔT = 0 the net axial momentum transfer from the inclined and vertical faces vanishes by the second law (quote in KQ3); flow scales approximately linearly with ΔT; "the main benefit of using the alternating diffuse-specular wall pattern is the generation of a wall-parallel temperature gradient at the tips of the ratchet"; fully diffuse ratchet gives "almost no flow"; maximum volume flow at 0.1 < Kn < 0.5 — [Shahabi, Baier, Roohi, Hardt, Sci. Rep. 7, 41412 (2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5269730/)
- Knudsen pump/ratchet designs (Donkov et al. 2011 with a specular inclined face; Würger 2011 fully diffuse ratchet) all drive flow "between two surfaces held at different temperatures"; DSMC studies find mass flow maximal near Kn ≈ 0.1 and reversing sign near Kn ≈ 1 — [arXiv:1703.05008 and HAL records (snippets)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05008)
- Cosine-law robustness: molecular-dynamics rebounds follow ρ(θ)dθ = ½cos θ dθ; the law is "asymptotic" and also arises from specular reflection in chaotic geometries — [Celestini & Mortessagne, arXiv:0708.1117](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.1117)
- Generalised Knudsen theory covering diffuse, partially diffuse and specular reflection exists (Nature Commun. 2023), but the article could not be opened (redirect to login) — [Nature Commun. 14, 7386 (2023) (not read)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-43104-6)

### Inferences
- Mapping to electrons: the Knudsen free-molecular regime (Kn ≫ 1, wall collisions only, no intermolecular collisions) is the exact classical analogue of the single-particle ballistic regime (l_e, l_ee ≫ W). The NASA equilibrium argument (equal arrival rates everywhere ⇒ zero net flow ⇒ A_1 W_12 = A_2 W_21) is the classical statement of T_SD(E) = T_DS(E): the shape-independence quoted above is the same shape-independence as unitarity. A Tesla-valve channel with specular walls therefore has equal molecular conductance in both directions at equal pressure, and the same holds for cosine-law walls (which Celestini–Mortessagne show are themselves the ergodic limit of specular chaos).
- Knudsen pumps are the gas analogue of thermoelectric/ratchet effects: they need a second thermodynamic force (ΔT) and then the asymmetric geometry picks the direction. The electronic analogue is a temperature gradient or an ac drive, not a dc two-terminal diode. This supports the KQ3 conclusion that mechanism (b) cannot rectify.
- The fluid Tesla valve (Re-dependent) sits at the other end: Kn ≪ 1 with intermolecular collisions dominating (hydrodynamic), which is where the electronic Tesla valves of KQ4 operate. The user's "Knudsen analogy" and "Tesla valve analogy" are therefore analogies for two mutually exclusive regimes; only the latter rectifies.

### Gaps
- Sharipov's reviews and Steckelmacher, Rep. Prog. Phys. 49, 1083 (1986) were not accessible; the explicit textbook form A_1 W_12 = A_2 W_21 is here inferred from the NASA equilibrium argument rather than quoted. Verify against Steckelmacher or Clausing (1932) if a formal citation is required.
- No source was found that treats a Tesla-valve geometry specifically in the free-molecular regime; the conclusion is drawn from the general shape-independent equilibrium argument.

## KQ6. Which claims that "specular reflections from an asymmetric boundary produce rectification" have been criticised or qualified, and what is the consensus mechanism?

### Takeaway
Within the geometric-diode literature itself the qualifications are: (i) uniform-field, perfectly specular, non-interacting Monte Carlo over-predicts asymmetry (self-consistent electrostatics "substantially counterbalance[s]" the geometric effect; measured A ≈ 1.1–1.4 vs higher simulated values); (ii) the asymmetry vanishes as MFP → 0 and decreases again at long MFP, so it is not a property of the boundary shape alone; (iii) in the Song/Fleischmann–Geisel debate, Büttiker and co-workers insist any nonlinear result must come from a self-consistent, gauge-invariant potential. The consensus mechanism is that the asymmetric geometry acts on a bias-shifted (collimated/drifted) non-equilibrium injected distribution and on the bias-dependent internal potential; the output is O(V²) with no linear-response component.

### Cited Findings
- "the simulations assume perfectly specular reflection at the device edges, and they do not model interactions between charge carriers. As a result, the simulated asymmetry ratios tend to exceed the measured values, which the authors say is common for geometric-diode simulations" — [Wang et al., Adv. Electron. Mater. (2024), doi:10.1002/aelm.202300695 (search snippet, not read)](https://advanced.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aelm.202300695)
- "the electrostatics plays a relevant role that tends to substantially counterbalance the geometrical effect"; maximum asymmetry bounded by W_d/W_n even in the uniform-field idealisation — [Truccolo et al. 2024](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- "the asymmetry approaches 1" as λ_MFP → 0; asymmetry peaks at finite λ_MFP and declines beyond; low-bias (≤0.1 V) reduction of asymmetry "not yet clear" — [Stearns & Moddel 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659/)
- Dragoman & Dragoman's quantum taper model obtains rectification by assuming the bias drops linearly across the taper: "We assume throughout this paper that a bias voltage V is applied across the tapered structure, such that V_in = 0, V_out = eV, and V_j = jeV/(N+1)"; "the number of emerging modes, and hence the current, which is proportional to the mode number in the Landauer theory, depends on the applied voltage: the current is high for one polarity and decreases to zero for the other polarity, as the number of outgoing modes decreases" — [Dragoman & Dragoman, arXiv:1208.3760 (J. Phys. D 46, 055306, 2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.3760)
- Fleischmann–Geisel characterise the Song explanation as "a phenomenological ansatz" and offer a non-self-consistent mode-counting alternative; Büttiker & Sánchez published a Comment (PRL 90, 119701, 2003) and the authors replied — [Fleischmann & Geisel 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0112305); [Comment record](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:4195)
- Christen–Büttiker: "many works on nonlinear transport consider only the non-interacting case or consider interactions which are not gauge invariant"; "Without considering the self-consistent shift ΔU_0 one would get a wrong result which is not gauge invariant" — [Christen & Büttiker 1996](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9604029)
- Song (consensus statement of mechanism): "the self-consistent field in the device causes a kind of collimation/decollimation effect, the extent of which can be determined by the magnitude of the excess velocity or the lead current"; "nonlinearity starts at any non-zero current"; and the geometric-asymmetry-only (zero-field) transmissions are symmetric, T_{L←S}(0) = T_{L←D}(0), T_{U←S}(0) = T_{U←D}(0) — [Song 1999](https://www.nano.physik.uni-muenchen.de/kotthaus/_assets/pdf/1999/99-09_Song_PRB.pdf)
- Auton et al. (graphene, 2016): "the rectifying functionality is based on the collimating effect of carriers induced by the applied current" — [Auton et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Geurs et al. show that in the ballistic regime of hBN-graphene the Tesla geometry gives D = 1 within the trivial gate-induced background — [Geurs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04862)

### Inferences
- Trajectory pictures ("funnel vs. bounce-back") in Zhu/Moddel 2012 and the Dragoman mode-counting argument are both instances of reasoning the user asked to flag: the first implicitly includes the field (carriers "modified slightly by applied electric fields"), the second explicitly imposes a linear potential drop; neither is a property of T(E) at equilibrium, and in both the asymmetry is zero at V = 0. The Dragoman result in particular depends entirely on the assumed V_j profile, which is not self-consistent and so not gauge invariant in the Christen–Büttiker sense; a different (equally arbitrary) choice of profile would give a different G_2.
- The qualitative consensus across Song (1999, 2002), Auton (2016), Stearns–Moddel (2021) and Truccolo (2024): the geometry is a transducer that converts an already non-equilibrium, direction-biased injected distribution into a direction-dependent transmission; the magnitude is controlled by λ_MFP/W_neck and is reduced by self-consistent screening. The strictly ballistic, zero-field limit gives no asymmetry.
- Experimental asymmetry ratios for two-terminal graphene geometric diodes are modest (A ≈ 1.1–1.4 at 1.5–2 V), consistent with the response being a weak G_2 V² correction rather than diode action.

### Gaps
- The Wang et al. 2024 criticism is from a search snippet; the paper was not read.
- No published paper was found that explicitly refutes a specific geometric-diode claim on reciprocity grounds; the critique is assembled from (i) the Büttiker-school gauge-invariance requirement and (ii) the MC literature's own limiting behaviours.
- The Büttiker–Sánchez Comment text (which would be the most direct published critique of a non-self-consistent ballistic-rectifier model) was not accessible.

## KQ7. Quantitative figures of merit (asymmetry ratio, responsivity, curvature coefficient, diodicity) and their relation to the nonlinearity needed

### Takeaway
The field uses: asymmetry ratio A = |I(+V)/I(−V)| at a stated V (geometric diodes, typical 1.1–1.4 at 1.5–2 V, theoretical ceiling W_d/W_n in the uniform-field ballistic model); zero-bias current responsivity β = ½ I''/I' in A/W (geometric diodes, 0.03–0.3 A/W); voltage responsivity V_dc/P_ac in mV/mW (ballistic rectifiers, up to 23,000 mV/mW); and diodicity Di = R_rev/R_fwd (Tesla valves, 1.05 graphene, up to ≈40 GaAs above threshold). All are expressions of G_2/G_1 (or higher-order terms); any linear-response diode figure is identically trivial (A = Di = 1, β = 0).

### Cited Findings
- Asymmetry: "A = |I_forward / I_backward|" at a specific V_DS; values 1.40 (50 nm neck) and 1.25 (100 nm neck) at V_DS = 2 V; zero-bias responsivity "Responsivity = ½ · I''(V)/I'(V)", "a measure of the output current produced for a given input power"; 0.0628 A/W (50 nm neck) vs 0.0285 A/W for the earlier exfoliated 75 nm device; >0.2 A/W at 0.5 V — [CVD-graphene geometric diode, PMC8399968](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399968/)
- Truccolo definition: A = I_DS(+V_DS)/I_DS(−V_DS) ≃ T_DS(+V_DS)/T_SD(−V_DS); large-V limit A → W_d/W_n; experimental A ≈ 1.3 (exfoliated, 1.5 V), ≈1.1 (CVD) — [Truccolo et al. 2024](https://iris.unimore.it/retrieve/f133a370-968e-4f53-beda-4a47d4b31aa3/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Moddel group defines zero-bias responsivity β(V) = ½ I''(V)/I'(V) evaluated at V = 0, often from a polynomial fit to I-V — [Moddel group MIIM responsivity page (snippet)](https://colorado.edu/faculty/moddel/content/responsivity-resistance-relationship-miim-diodes); zero-bias responsivity 0.3 A/W quoted for a graphene geometric diode — [Moddel group page (snippet)](https://colorado.edu/faculty/moddel/content/geometric-diodes-optical-rectennas)
- Curvature coefficient k_v (≡ γ = I''/I', units V⁻¹) is the figure of merit used for detector/rectifier diodes; ≈33 V⁻¹ for an optimised tunnel diode, ≈40 V⁻¹ Schottky, ≥70 V⁻¹ Ge — [IJEEE 2024 (snippet)](https://ijeee.iust.ac.ir/article-1-2738-en.html)
- Voltage responsivity (ballistic rectifier): "The responsivity is determined by the output d.c. voltage divided by the input a.c. power"; 23,000 mV/mW; NEP 0.64 pW/Hz^½ — [Auton et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4895026/)
- Diodicity: Di = R_reverse/R_forward (electron Tesla valve, "a standard characteristics of asymmetric resistors"; Di ≈ 40) — [Sarypov et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16443); D = R_f/R_b with dc four-terminal resistances, D ≈ 1.05 viscous signal; "Other possible definitions of the diodicity, such as the ratio of differential resistances, are discussed in the Supplementary Information" — [Geurs et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04862); fluid: Di = Hg_R/Hg_F (Hagen-number ratio), Di ≈ 2 plateau — [Nguyen et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8128925)
- Quadratic small-signal law for the ballistic rectifier, V_LU ∝ I_SD² with coefficient given analytically (KQ2), and dc output linear in ac power — [Song 2002](https://www.nano.physik.uni-muenchen.de/kotthaus/_assets/pdf/2002/02_11_Song_Appl_Phys.A.pdf)

### Inferences
- Relations to the expansion I = G_1 V + G_2 V² + G_3 V³: I'(0) = G_1, I''(0) = 2G_2, hence β(0) = ½I''/I' = G_2/G_1 and γ = I''/I' = 2G_2/G_1. The asymmetry ratio to leading order is A(V) = (G_1 V + G_2 V²)/(G_1 V − G_2 V²) ≈ 1 + 2(G_2/G_1)V, so A = 1.4 at 2 V corresponds to G_2/G_1 ≈ 0.1 V⁻¹ (β ≈ 0.1 A/W), consistent with the reported pairs (1.40; 0.063 A/W). Diodicity in the Tesla-valve sense, Di = R(−I)/R(+I), is the same ratio evaluated at large current where the expansion no longer holds (GaAs Di ≈ 40 is a threshold phenomenon, not a G_2 effect).
- For a Tesla-valve-shaped two-terminal ballistic channel, the achievable figure is bounded by the geometric-diode results for the same transport physics: A ≲ W_shoulder/W_neck in the uniform-field idealisation, reduced further by self-consistent screening and by finite MFP; realistic graphene values A ≈ 1.1–1.4, β ≲ 0.3 A/W, γ ≲ 0.6 V⁻¹ — roughly two orders of magnitude below Schottky/tunnel-diode curvature coefficients.
- Rectenna-style figures (β, cutoff RC) rather than A are the right metrics if the intended use is high-frequency detection; the low capacitance (attofarads) rather than the nonlinearity is the geometric diode's selling point in that literature.

### Gaps
- An authoritative definition of the curvature coefficient γ = I''/I' from a primary rectenna/diode source was not fetched; the ½ factor convention for β is from Moddel-group snippets and the PMC8399968 equation, not from the J. Phys. D 2013 paper.
- Noise-equivalent power and responsivity of the 2025 "thermal noise rectification" Tesla-valve-type device could not be verified.
