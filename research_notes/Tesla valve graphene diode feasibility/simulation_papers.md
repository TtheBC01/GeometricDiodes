## Key Question 1 — Source 1: Stearns & Moddel, "Simulation of Z-Shaped Graphene Geometric Diodes Using Particle-in-Cell Monte Carlo Method in the Quasi-Ballistic Regime", Nanomaterials 11(9), 2361 (2021)

### Takeaway
A MATLAB particle-in-cell (PIC) Monte Carlo with Drude-type momentum randomisation (exponentially distributed collision times, τ = λMFP/vF), perfectly specular edges, a 2D in-plane Poisson solve on a triangular mesh with a uniform neutralising background, and macroparticles initialised at random positions/directions (not injected from Fermi-Dirac reservoirs). For a Z-diode with neck 100 nm, shoulder 1 µm, λMFP = 500 nm, it predicts a non-monotonic asymmetry vs λMFP peaking near λMFP ≈ 600 nm at ±0.5 V; the only validation is a Drude-conductance match on a 10 µm × 2.5 µm strip. No numerical asymmetry values are given in the text (figures only).

### Cited Findings
- Authors: John Stearns and Garret Moddel, Department of Electrical, Computer, and Energy Engineering, University of Colorado Boulder; Moddel corresponding. Published 11 Sept 2021, DOI 10.3390/nano11092361 — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659); [MDPI landing](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-4991/11/9/2361)
- Transport model (Sec. 2): macroparticles each represent "100 or 200 individual electrons"; each is "initiated with a randomly directed velocity vector with magnitude equal to vF"; collision times "are sampled from an exponential decay distribution where e−t/τ represents the probability that an electron picked at random will have no collision during the next time interval t, and τ = λMFP/vF represents the average momentum relaxation time". On collision the particle gets "a new randomly-directed velocity with magnitude vF" (isotropic, fully momentum-randomising, elastic) and the residual negative counter value is carried over so "on average, the particles will travel approximately a distance of λMFP between collisions" — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Carrier dynamics use a Newtonian effective mass, not massless Dirac kinematics: m* = h²n/(4π m0² vF²) (Eq. 2), giving m* = 0.02 for n = 7.2 × 10¹¹ cm⁻²; "The field exerts a force on the particle, changing its velocity over a small time-step dt" — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Edge model: "If a particle collides with a device boundary, it is reflected specularly and continues propagating." No diffuse component or specularity parameter — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Injection / contacts: particles are "scattered within the device at random locations" at t = 0; "When a particle crosses one of the electrode boundaries, a counter, Q ... is incremented or decremented accordingly" (in the predecessor Zhu-style MC "another carrier is injected from the opposite electrode"). No Fermi–Dirac reservoir, no cosine/flux weighting, no drifted distribution; all carriers have |v| = vF (the paper states "all of the carriers are assumed to have energy given by EF with negligible spread", EF = 100 meV ≈ 4 kBT) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Electrostatics: "The input voltage is set as a boundary condition between the two electrodes. MATLAB's PDE package is used to solve Poisson's equation for the potential at every mesh node." Charge is assigned to the three surrounding triangle nodes by distance weighting and the same weights interpolate the field back (CIC-like on a triangular mesh); "There is assumed to be a homogenous distribution of background positive charge that maintains charge neutrality in the device as a whole." The Poisson solve is in-plane 2D (triangular mesh in the device outline) — no substrate/gate dielectric or 3D domain is described; the gate enters only through n = ε0εrVg/(tq) with Vg = 10 V, t = 300 nm SiO2, εr = 3.9 → n = 7.2 × 10¹¹ cm⁻² — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Numerical parameters: dt = τ/10 for all simulations; "maximum mesh size was set to 100 nm at the shoulders and 40 nm at the neck" (strip benchmark: 250 nm uniform); macroparticle count "hundreds" (Fig. 1 shows 20 "for clarity"); convergence procedure: "we repeated simulations for decreasing dt and mesh size to find values for each where the resulting I(V) remained approximately constant"; "a sufficient number of macroparticles were used so that the results did not change with a further increase". Total simulated time Nt·dt not stated. Current I = n A q Q/(Nmacro Nt dt) (Eq. 1) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Stated numerical pitfalls: too large dt lets the collision counter overshoot → "artifactual increase in mobility" and overstated current; too coarse mesh understates the field at constrictions → lower current — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Geometry (Sec. 3.2, Fig. 4): shoulder (electrode) width ds = 1 µm, neck dn = 100 nm, λMFP = 500 nm, V swept −0.5 to +0.5 V, for both the inverse-arrowhead and the Z-diode; Z-arm lengths/overall length are not given in text (figure only). Z-diode was "proposed by Zhu" (PhD thesis) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Asymmetry metric: Asymmetry = |I(+V0)/I(−V0)| (Eq. 3). Results: Z-diode "exhibits a more nonlinear I(V) curve and a higher current asymmetry above 0.1 V" than the inverse arrowhead but is "less asymmetric at 0.1 V and below"; "It is possible that it is an artifact of the simulator at low currents" (Fig. 5 caption). Asymmetry vs λMFP at 0.5 V: → 1 as λMFP → 0, and "the peak asymmetry appears to occur around λpeak ≈ 600 nm, with asymmetry dropping for λMFP > λpeak"; "The value of λpeak is likely to change based on the applied voltage and the diode shape, and it may not exist at all for certain geometries." No numeric asymmetry values appear in the text — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Drude benchmark (Sec. 3.1, Fig. 2): rectangular strip 10 µm × 2.5 µm, 250 nm uniform mesh, n = 7.2 × 10¹¹ cm⁻², m* = 0.02, λMFP = 500 nm; "The simulated result agrees well with the analytical solution [Drude]". No quantitative error given — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Stated limitations (Sec. 4–5): assumes EF ≫ kBT (here EF = 100 meV, ×4 kBT); neglects wave effects (de Broglie λ ≈ 40 nm, k = 150 µm⁻¹; "interference effects ... become important for device dimensions below the 100 nm scale"); neglects velocity saturation/optical phonons ("saturation effects become strong for applied fields around 0.5 V/µm"; "should not be used with applied voltages exceeding the 1 V scale" for micron devices). Data on FigShare 10.6084/m9.figshare.16606217.v1 — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)
- Independent characterisation of this code by Nguyen et al.: "Stearns and Moddel predicted ~5 with geometry similar to device A, where the drift velocity was parallel to the direction of the applied voltage" (i.e. the PIC-MC class of models tends to predict asymmetries far above the ≤2 measured) — [Nguyen et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41699-021-00269-2)
- Truccolo et al. 2024 characterise it as "a particle-in-cell algorithm in conjunction with an electron momentum relaxation mechanism. Our approach differs from that of [22] because we add the full 3-D electrostatic" — [Truccolo TED 2024, p. 1295](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because particles start uniformly inside the device with isotropic velocities and the counter-based current is a drift-current estimator, the Stearns–Moddel scheme is effectively a closed-box Drude drift simulation with specular walls, not a contact-injection (Landauer-type) simulation. Its asymmetry arises from field-driven drift plus wall reflections; it has no Fermi-level/contact-reservoir physics and no Pauli blocking.
- The 2D in-plane Poisson solve with a uniform neutralising background is not equivalent to a gated graphene sheet: the gate/oxide image charge that screens accumulations at the neck (the "electrostatic attraction" in Truccolo et al.) is absent, so the near-neck field is not the same physical object as in a 3D gated solve.
- The non-monotonic asymmetry vs λMFP (peak ≈ 600 nm for a 100 nm neck, ratio ≈ 6) is a model-specific result that depends on reverse-bias multi-bounce paths; its robustness to specularity, Poisson dimensionality and injection model is untested.

### Gaps
- No numeric I–V or asymmetry values are present in the text; the figures (and FigShare data) would need to be read to extract them.
- Macroparticle counts, Nt, and total simulated time are never quantified.
- Z-diode arm lengths and channel length are given only graphically.

## Key Question 2 — Source 2: Truccolo, Palestri, Esseni, Boscolo, Midrio, "Comprehensive Analysis of Graphene Geometric Diodes: Role of Geometrical Asymmetry and Electrostatic Effects", IEEE Trans. Electron Devices 71(2), 1294–1301 (Feb 2024), DOI 10.1109/TED.2023.3347209

### Takeaway
A ballistic (no scattering), perfectly specular, electrons-only semiclassical MC with analytic Dirac-cone free flights on a triangulated tensor mesh, Fermi–Dirac contact injection, coupled to a 3D non-linear Poisson FDM (graphene + 100 nm oxide + 20 nm air + back-gate). The field-free/uniform-field ("geometry-only") case does produce asymmetry (A ≈ 1.05–1.15 at low bias, up to ~3 at 200 mV for αd = 84.3°, Wd/Wn = 4 upper bound), but full 3D electrostatics roughly halves it and can drive A below 1 (sign reversal) for shallow funnel angles; conclusion: uniform-field/geometry-only analysis "tends to overestimate the rectifying capabilities", and electrostatics "can cancel or even reverse" geometric rectification.

### Cited Findings
- Authors/affiliations: D. Truccolo, D. Esseni, S. Boscolo (DPIA, Univ. of Udine); P. Palestri (Udine, now Univ. of Modena and Reggio Emilia); M. Midrio (Udine and CNIT). Horizon 2020 GreEnergy grant 101006963. Received 8 Dec 2023, published 8 Jan 2024 — [TED 2024, p. 1294](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Scope statement: "we will limit our analysis to ballistic transport and perfectly specular reflections at the graphene edges. Namely, we deliberately neglect the influence of momentum randomizing scattering mechanisms, such as phonon or edge-roughness scattering" and "we assume a robustly n-type graphene, where hole transport can be safely neglected" — [p. 1295](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Free-flight kinematics: ε = ħvF|k|, ds/dt = vF k/|k|, dk/dt = −qE/ħ; k(t) = k0 + Rt with R = −qE/ħ; a closed-form trajectory s(t) (Eq. 3, involving √(A0²+2A1t+A2²t²) and a log term) valid while E is constant, i.e. inside one triangle of the mesh ("similar to the simplex Monte Carlo"); flight duration = shortest intersection time of the analytic trajectory with the triangle edges — [p. 1295–1296](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Injection: "At defined time steps, carriers are injected from the device terminals according to the Fermi level of the terminal"; injected current Ĩα = (2qWc/(πħ)²vF) ∫0^∞ dE E/(1+e^{(E−Efα)/kBT}) (Eq. 4) with Efα = q(φc − Vα), φc the average electrostatic potential along the contact line; only conduction band injected (E > 0); "When a particle hits a device terminal, it is absorbed. If hitting another boundary, it gets reflected." Net current = injected − absorbed at each contact; VD = +VDS/2, VS = −VDS/2 — [p. 1296](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf). (Angular distribution of injected carriers is specified in the thesis as P(β) = ½cos β — see KQ3.)
- Poisson: 3D non-uniform-grid FDM; graphene plane discretised Nx ≈ Ny ≈ 100–200 for Wc ≈ Lc ≈ 100–300 nm → dx ≈ dy ≈ 1 nm; Δz = 1 nm (10 points) around graphene, ≈10 nm further away; tox = 100 nm, tair = 20 nm (verified large enough), Nz ≈ 20–30 → NxNyNz ≈ 10⁶ unknowns. Graphene treated as a δ(z) sheet (field discontinuity; no graphene dielectric constant), verified equivalent to a 0.34 nm slab. Neumann BC on all borders except back-gate (Dirichlet Vbk); metal work function assumed = graphene electron affinity. No doping or fixed charges — [p. 1296–1297](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Coupling scheme: non-linear Poisson ∇·(ε∇φ^{k+1}) = δ(z) q n^{k} exp(q(φ^{k+1}−φ^{k})/kBT) (Eq. 5) solved by Newton–Raphson, chosen because the linear scheme "necessitate[s] an extremely frequent MC-Poisson iteration" and plasma oscillations; frozen-field MC between Poisson solves means "The nonlinear Poisson scheme is not affected by self-forces" — [p. 1296–1297](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Numerics (Sec. III-B): alternate 10⁵ MC steps with one 3D non-linear Poisson solve; Δt ≈ dx/vF ≈ 0.1 fs; pinj = 20 virtual electrons injected per contact per step with weight w = Ĩα Δt/(q pinj) ("We found pinj = 20 to be a good tradeoff between accuracy and simulation time"); initial potential guess from the 1D capacitor solution Eq. 7; "the simulation is stopped when IDS stabilizes" — [p. 1298](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Geometry (Fig. 1, fixed): Wc = 100 nm (contact width), Wd = 40 nm (funnel base), Wn = 10 nm (neck), εox = 3.7ε0, εair = ε0, tox = 100 nm; angle αd varied via Ld (7.6°, 45°, 69.4°, 84.3°); Vbk = 3.38 V → n = 6.7 × 10¹¹ cm⁻², p = 1.6 × 10¹⁰ cm⁻² — [p. 1297](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Metrics: transmission coefficients Tij = fraction of electrons injected by j absorbed by i; current asymmetry A = IDS(+VDS)/IDS(−VDS) ≈ TDS(+VDS)/TSD(−VDS) (Eq. 9) — [p. 1297–1298](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Uniform-field (geometry-only) results: at Ex = 0, TDS = TSD = Wn/Wc = 0.1; large-|VDS| limit A → Wd/Wn (= 4 here) but "an unrealistic source–drain voltage VDS > 10 V must be applied to have A approach its upper limit"; "a rather weak asymmetry (A ≃ 1.05–1.15) is achieved in the low-voltage regime" (Fig. 4b, thesis Fig. 5.3b axis spans A = 1–3 up to 200 mV); optimal αd = 84.3° found numerically; A increases as n decreases (kF = √(2πn) smaller → easier alignment); A → 1 when n ≈ p — [p. 1297–1298](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Self-consistent results: a symmetric control device gives A = 1 for all VDS ("to check the code"); with full electrostatics TSD is no longer flat for VDS < 0 — field concentrates at the neck tips and "Electrons are 'attracted' toward the neck, and the transmission coefficient TSD can greatly exceed the pure geometrical constraint Wn/Wc" ("electrostatic attraction"); "the asymmetry coefficient has almost halved when the full electrostatics is considered" (thesis Fig. 5.5c axis: A ≈ 0.9–1.4 up to 200 mV); for αd = 7.6° "A becomes slightly lower than one" (sign reversal) because electrostatic attraction exceeds geometric enhancement; TSD, TDS deviate from Wn/Wc even at VDS = 0; A decreases with increasing Vbk (3.38, 7.2, 30 V) and with increasing substrate εr — [p. 1299–1300](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Comparison with experiment: "a direct comparison with the experimental results in [15] is not possible because ballistic transport conditions are assumed", but qualitatively consistent (A falls with Wn; Wang et al. measured 1.25–1.40 at 2 V on µm devices vs simulated comparable values at 200 mV on ~100 nm devices, i.e. similar fields; A falls with gate voltage as in Nguyen et al.) — [p. 1300](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Conclusions verbatim: "the evaluation of the device rectification based only on geometry asymmetry is incomplete. In fact, electrostatic effects, in particular the charge-induced electric field near the diode's neck, influence significantly the device operation and can cancel or even reverse the rectification due to the geometric asymmetry"; design info: "1) the analysis of the device under a uniform field condition tends to overestimate the rectifying capabilities of the geometric diode and 2) the asymmetry coefficient tends to improve as the funnel angle αd increases, with a maximum asymmetry occurring for αd ≃ 85°" — [p. 1300](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Quantum effects dismissed qualitatively: "the quantum billiard view of transport in ballistic devices boils down to semi-classical ballistic transport (i.e., classical billiard balls)" at room temperature — [p. 1295](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Future work named: local and remote phonons, edge roughness, high-frequency behaviour — [p. 1300](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)

### Inferences
- Direct answer to "does asymmetry vanish or change sign without electrostatics?": No — without self-consistent electrostatics (uniform field) the purely geometric, specular, ballistic model gives A > 1 for all funnel angles (overestimated); it is *with* electrostatics that A is halved and can become < 1. So a geometry-only MC does predict rectification, but overpredicts it and misses a sign-reversal regime.
- The pure-geometry asymmetry in this model exists only because the uniform in-plane field Ex bends trajectories (k(t) = k0 + Rt); at exactly zero field TDS = TSD = Wn/Wc by construction. A field-free, zero-bias specular ballistic model therefore gives exactly A = 1; asymmetry needs the bias field acting on trajectories.
- With Wd/Wn = 4 the theoretical ceiling A → Wd/Wn is approached only at VDS > 10 V — the practical room-temperature asymmetry from geometry alone is modest (≈1.05–1.3 at ≤ 200 mV).
- Implementation-relevant: the Udine code uses an event-driven free flight inside mesh triangles (analytic trajectory, shortest edge-intersection time, bisection root find), NGP charge assignment (thesis), frozen-field MC between non-linear Poisson updates, and contact injection with statistical weights — a template directly applicable to an event-driven C++/CUDA design.

### Gaps
- Numeric A values at specific biases are only in figures; the text gives ranges (1.05–1.15 low bias uniform field; "almost halved" self-consistent; A < 1 at αd = 7.6°).
- The injection angular distribution and energy sampling are not stated in the TED paper itself (they are in the thesis, KQ3).
- No explicit particle-count or mesh convergence study is reported in the paper beyond "pinj = 20 ... good tradeoff" and "tair = 20 nm ... sufficiently large".

## Key Question 3 — Source 3: Truccolo PhD thesis "Modeling of graphene geometrical rectifiers", Univ. of Udine, XXXVI cycle (2020–2023), supervisors M. Midrio and P. Palestri

### Takeaway
The thesis is the full documentation of the Udine simulator: synchronous ensemble MC with analytic Dirac free flights on a triangulated tensor mesh (time step 10⁻¹⁶ s shown accurate vs 10⁻¹⁷ s linear stepping), specular or random edge reflections, self-scattering with isotropic LA + LO intrinsic phonons, inelastic anisotropic SiO2 remote phonons (55.6 meV), elastic Gaussian-correlated random potential (Lcorr = 1 nm, Vrnd fit), approximate Fermi–Dirac rejection for Pauli blocking, Fermi–Dirac energy sampling + cosine (½cos β) angular injection, NGP charge assignment, 3D non-linear Poisson (FDM, Newton–Raphson, 10⁻⁷ V tolerance) verified against COMSOL and against analytic capacitor/ballistic-conductance results, convergence judged by current stabilisation and the solenoidal (IDS = −ISD) condition, plus a preliminary FDTD-MC (A–Φ formulation, A = 0) showing A ≈ 1.2 at 2 THz vanishing by 10 THz.

### Cited Findings
- Abstract: "a self-consistent Monte Carlo simulator has been developed, coupling the charge transport in the graphene layer with 3-D electrostatics arising from the gate voltage under the oxide substrate"; four-terminal rectifier modelled by "Monte Carlo transport (without self-consistency) and Landauer-Büttiker formalism"; ongoing FDTD-MC work — [thesis, p. i–ii](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)
- Ensemble MC (§2.1, p. 16): synchronous time grid ti = iΔt; free flights interrupted by (i) mesh-element transitions, (ii) device-boundary interactions, (iii) scattering events — [thesis](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)
- Free flight (§2.2, p. 17–19): analytic trajectory Eq. 2.5 for constant E in each triangle; limit cases: A2 = 0 (zero field → linear Eq. 2.4) and trajectory parallel to E ("we increase the angle trajectory of 10⁻⁶ deg"); Fig. 2.3: analytic solution with dtf = 10⁻¹⁶ s matches linear scheme only at 10⁻¹⁷ s ("the linear approximation requires a time step at least an order of magnitude smaller to achieve the same accuracy"); linear scheme would otherwise need dtf ≈ 10⁻¹⁸–10⁻¹⁹ s — [thesis p. 18–19](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)
- Mesh transitions (§2.2.1, p. 19–20): intersection of the analytic trajectory with the three triangle edge lines solved "using the bisection algorithm"; shortest time selected; particle moved to intersection, field updated, remaining dtf − tint continued; multiple elements per step allowed — [thesis](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)
- Boundaries (§2.2.2, p. 21; §2.7, p. 31): "the particle's wave vector is updated according to the type of reflection, which may be either specular or random"; two boundary types: reflecting (define shape) and injecting (ohmic contacts modelled as reservoirs in thermal equilibrium just outside the domain; Neumann BC on Poisson at contacts) — [thesis](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)

- Scattering mechanisms (§2.4, p. 24–28): intrinsic LA acoustic (quasi-elastic, equipartition, SLA = ¼·2πq Dac² kBT/(ρ vs² ħ)·Dos/(NsNv); ρ = 7.6 × 10⁻⁷ kg/m², vs = 2 × 10⁴ m/s, Dac = 14 eV; factor ¼ from averaging the (1+cosθ)/2 spinor overlap) and LO optical (ELO = 152 meV, DLO = 10¹¹ eV/m, isotropic, emission/absorption); remote SO phonons from SiO2 (ωSO from ε2(ω)+ε1 = 0; only the 55.6 meV TO mode, d = 0 graphene–oxide distance, inelastic, anisotropic matrix element with (1+cosθ)/2 and e^{−2Qd}); random potential (Urnd = Vrnd Δ(r), Gaussian autocorrelation, |Δ(Q)|² = πL² e^{−(QL/2)²}, L = 1 nm fixed, Vrnd tunable, elastic) — [thesis](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf). No ionised-impurity (Coulomb) scattering is implemented as such; defects are lumped into the random potential.
- Pauli blocking (§2.5, p. 28–29): rejection using an approximate local Fermi–Dirac f(ε, r) = 1/(e^{(ε−qVel(r))/kBT}+1); if f > r3 the final state is rejected — [thesis](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf). (The 2026 paper refines this to a local quasi-Fermi level and electron temperature, see KQ4.)
- Gate band diagram (§2.6, p. 29–31): χgr ≈ 4.5 eV quoted, but the printed MATLAB snippet uses Chi_g = 4.1, W_m = 3.5, vf = 8.8e5 m/s, εox = 3.7 ε0, tox = 100 nm, T = 300 K, solving φgr − Vbk + χ − Φm + (tox q/εox)(n−p) = 0 with fzero; the result is the Poisson initial guess — [thesis p. 30–31](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)

- Estimators (§2.8, p. 34–36): current from weighted particle crossings at contacts, averaged over the second half of Nsim steps; charge density by Nearest-Grid-Point (NGP) assignment, also second-half averaged; "the solenoidal condition is expected to hold, meaning the currents estimated along the two boundaries should have equal magnitude but opposite signs" — [thesis](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)

## Key Question 4 — Related modelling papers (Truccolo 2026; Wang 2021; Zhu 2013/thesis; Dragoman; Custer/White; Nguyen; Mencarelli; Mohebbi; others)

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- There are two distinct semiclassical MC lineages: (a) Moddel group (Zhu → Stearns → Wang): Drude PIC, closed-box random initialisation, effective-mass Newtonian dynamics, 2D in-plane Poisson, specular walls, isotropic momentum reset at exponential times; (b) Udine group (Truccolo/Palestri/Midrio): Boltzmann-MC with contact reservoirs, Dirac kinematics, physical phonon/roughness rates, 3D gated Poisson. Only (b) has been quantitatively benchmarked against a fabricated device (fit parameter Vrnd).
- All semiclassical models that include specular edges and a bias field predict A > 1; every comparison with experiment finds the model overpredicts (Nguyen: theory 5–10 vs measured ≤ 2; Truccolo 2026: ballistic 1.20 vs measured ~1.0x, reconciled only by adding scattering). This is the main "validation" lesson for a new simulator: a geometry-only, specular, ballistic model is an upper bound, not a prediction.
- Coherent/tight-binding studies (Mencarelli, Mohebbi) report asymmetries in the same 1.0–1.4 range for 4–10 nm necks, i.e. they do not contradict the semiclassical picture for room-temperature devices; no study was found that uses them to argue the semiclassical picture is qualitatively wrong.

## Key Question 5 — Cross-cutting: drifted/shifted Fermi distribution, injection angular weighting, 3D Poisson with substrate/gate, and the neck-width-to-MFP ratio

### Takeaway
Only the Udine code injects from equilibrium Fermi–Dirac reservoirs with a flux-weighted cosine angular law and solves 3D Poisson with oxide + gate; the Moddel code uses no reservoirs at all (closed box, monoenergetic vF, isotropic directions, 2D Poisson with neutral background); the Si-nanowire MC injects a thermal Maxwellian with an explicit drift shift and uniform position/angle. No graphene MC paper injects a drifted (shifted) Fermi distribution — the bias enters through contact Fermi levels and the self-consistent field. The governing scaling parameter in the Udine work is MFP relative to the funnel length Ld (not the neck width): ballistic asymmetry is retained for MFP/Ld ≥ 1, lost below 10⁻², and "functional" above 0.1; the neck width sets the geometric ceiling A → Wd/Wn and A falls monotonically with Wn in both uniform-field and self-consistent cases.

### Cited Findings
- Udine injection: energy from the cumulative Fermi-weighted DOS of the contact reservoir (Emax = 1 eV grid); angle P(β) = ½cos β, sampled via x = arcsin(2r − 1); random position along the contact; electron/hole type by injection-current ratio; weight pw = (Ĩel+Ĩho)dt/(q Pinj) — [thesis §2.7, p. 32–34](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf). Contact Fermi level Efα = q(φc − Vα) uses the average contact-line potential from the Poisson solve, so the "drift" is produced self-consistently rather than by shifting the injected distribution — [TED 2024 p. 1296](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Moddel injection: none from contacts; macroparticles "scattered within the device at random locations" with random directions at |v| = vF; monoenergetic (EF ≫ kBT assumption) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659)

- Neck width: TED 2024 Fig. 7 — A vs Wn (10, 20, 30 nm) at Vbk = 3.38 V, Wd = 40 nm, decreasing with Wn in both uniform-field (axis to A ≈ 2) and self-consistent (axis to A ≈ 1.3) cases; theoretical ceiling A → Wd/Wn — [TED 2024 p. 1298–1300](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf); [thesis Fig. 5.7, p. 68](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf)
- MFP scaling: "the ratio between the mean free path and the characteristic length Ld ... rather than mobility itself, is the fundamental parameter"; MFP ≈ µ(h/2e)√(n/π); ballistic A preserved for MFP/Ld ≥ 1, A → 1 at MFP/Ld < 10⁻²; functional requirement MFP/Ld > 0.1 — [TED 2026 p. 2699, 2701](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/0f18e76d-048c-4823-a490-9b63acfa3257/Impact_of_Scattering_on_Graphene_Geometric_Diodes_A_Comparative_Analysis_Between_Simulations_and_Experiments.pdf)
- Moddel group: for a Z-diode with dn = 100 nm, ds = 1 µm, asymmetry at 0.5 V peaks at λMFP ≈ 600 nm (λ/dn ≈ 6) and falls for longer λMFP; Wang et al. simulate λMFP = 70 nm with dn = 50 nm (λ/dn ≈ 1.4) and get I–V consistent with experiment — [PMC Stearns](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659); [PMC Wang](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399968/)
- Experimental anchor for neck/MFP: Wang 2021 (CVD, MFP ≈ 70 nm) A = 1.40 at Wn = 50 nm, 1.25 at 100 nm, ≈1 for ≥150 nm; Nguyen (hBN, MFP ≈ 0.7 µm) up to 1.66 at Wn = 175–200 nm — [TED 2024 p. 1294–1295](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a C++/CUDA event-driven design, the Udine recipe is the one with demonstrated acceptance tests: cosine-weighted Fermi–Dirac reservoir injection at contacts, analytic per-cell free flights, frozen-field MC between non-linear 3D Poisson solves (10⁵ MC steps of ~0.1 fs per solve), NGP deposition, second-half time averaging, and the solenoidal-current check. The Moddel scheme's closed-box initialisation and 2D Poisson are simpler but physically weaker (no reservoirs, no gate screening).
- The two groups disagree on the trend with MFP at fixed geometry: Udine finds monotonic improvement up to the ballistic plateau (A constant for MFP/Ld ≥ 1), whereas Stearns–Moddel find a maximum at finite λMFP ≈ 6 × neck width. The difference plausibly comes from the Moddel model's lack of reservoirs/absorbing-contact physics (particles persist and multi-bounce indefinitely) and 2D electrostatics; this is a concrete discriminating test for a new code.

### Gaps
- No paper injects an explicitly drifted Fermi–Dirac distribution for graphene; no paper quantifies the sensitivity of A to the injection angular law (cosine vs uniform).
- The neck-to-MFP ratio is never isolated as the control parameter in the Udine work (they use Ld); the Moddel work gives only one geometry's λ-sweep.

## Key Question 6 — Specular vs diffuse boundaries; is zero asymmetry ever reported for purely specular, elastic, ballistic transport without electrostatics?

### Takeaway
Every simulation paper found uses perfectly specular edges; none reports a quantitative diffuse-vs-specular comparison for a graphene geometric diode (the Udine code has a "random" reflection switch but publishes no results with it; Nguyen et al. invoke partial diffusivity only qualitatively; White et al. assume atomically smooth specular Si surfaces). On zero asymmetry: in the Udine uniform-field model, A = 1 exactly at zero field (TDS = TSD = Wn/Wc) and the symmetric control device gives A = 1 at all biases; with a finite uniform bias field and specular ballistic transport, A > 1 always — no paper reports zero or negligible asymmetry for specular elastic ballistic transport under bias without electrostatics. The only reported sign reversal/cancellation is *caused by* adding self-consistent electrostatics (αd = 7.6°).

### Cited Findings
- Udine: "perfectly specular reflections at the graphene edges" (TED 2024) — [p. 1295](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf); thesis: "reflected, either specularly or randomly" option exists — [thesis p. 31](https://air.uniud.it/bitstream/11390/1307204/2/truccolo_phd_thesis_final.pdf); "edge-roughness scattering" explicitly deferred to future work — [TED 2024 p. 1300](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Moddel: "reflected specularly" (Stearns 2021; Wang 2021) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8470659); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8399968/)
- White/Cahoon: "idealized surfaces that are atomically smooth and defect free ... specular reflection"; surface reflection "probabilistically independent from a scattering event in the bulk" — [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10433696)
- Zero-field symmetry: "Ex = 0. In this case, a symmetrical transport condition is expected, resulting in TDS = TSD = Wn/Wc"; symmetric device "asymmetry coefficient is 1 for all VDS" — [TED 2024 p. 1297, 1299](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Under uniform field, specular ballistic A > 1 for all αd tested (7.6°–84.3°), A ≈ 1.05–1.15 at low bias — [TED 2024 p. 1298](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Sign reversal only with electrostatics: at αd = 7.6° "A becomes slightly lower than one ... entirely due to the electric field, which is no longer uniform" — [TED 2024 p. 1299](https://iris.unimore.it/bitstream/11380/1330448/1/truccoloTED24.pdf)
- Diffuse edges invoked qualitatively to explain theory > experiment: "the reflection from the graphene edge may be diffused partially due to non-uniformly etched edge lines ... These diffusion phenomena may reduce the rectification efficiency strongly as shown in the angular distribution effect (Supplementary Figs. 10, 11)"; and the hypothesis that high current/field "renders reflections at the edges specular" via trapped edge charge — [Nguyen et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41699-021-00269-2)
- Truccolo 2026 lumps roughness into the random-potential bulk scattering (Vrnd) rather than an edge model: random scattering "introduced to model roughness-induced scattering at the graphene surface but also to model the cumulative effect of other elastic scattering processes" — [p. 2698](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/0f18e76d-048c-4823-a490-9b63acfa3257/Impact_of_Scattering_on_Graphene_Geometric_Diodes_A_Comparative_Analysis_Between_Simulations_and_Experiments.pdf)

### Inferences
- A geometry-only specular ballistic MC *will* show rectification under bias (because the in-plane field curves trajectories and the funnel converts field alignment into transmission excess), but the literature consensus is that this is an overestimate: 3D electrostatics halves it or reverses it, and momentum-randomising scattering removes it once MFP/Ld < 0.1. No published result supports the hypothesis that specular elastic ballistic transport by itself gives zero asymmetry under bias.
- A specularity sweep (e.g. Soffer/Fuchs-type p-parameter or Nguyen's Supplementary "angular distribution effect") is an open, publishable gap that a new simulator could fill; the only related hint is Nguyen's supplementary material (not accessed).

### Gaps
- No quantitative specular-vs-diffuse asymmetry data exist in any accessed paper; Nguyen et al.'s Supplementary Figs. 10–11 (angular-distribution effect) were not accessed.
- No paper tests a zero-bias AC (true ratchet) response in a semiclassical specular model except the preliminary Udine FDTD-MC (ballistic, A ≈ 1.2 at 2 THz, vanishing by 10 THz, with vector potential neglected).
